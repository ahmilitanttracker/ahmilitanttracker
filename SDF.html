The Syrian Democratic Forces

Updated: 2/9/25

Overview

The Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) is a Kurdish-led coalition of forces located in North-Eastern Syria. The largest and oldest group inside of the SDF is the YPG , a Syrian branch of the PKK , the largest regional left-wing Kurdish militant organization. The YPG was established during the start of the Syrian Civil War, when the nearly vacated northern Syria regions were poised to come under control of islamist groups. When the YPG was threatened by the rapidly growing Islamic State in 2014, they joined up with moderate Arab groups to form the SDF. During the war against ISIS , the SDF would become the most reliable local partner of the American coalition. Despite this, Turkey and Turkish aligned opposition groups would often fight the SDF, which they saw as a direct extension of the hostile and anti-Turkish PKK . The SDF established the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria, a de-facto independent system encompassing much of "Rojava". Since the fall of Assad , the SDF have been open to integrating with the Damascus Government , despite ongoing clashes with the Turkish -backed SNA . The SDF are designated as a terrorist group by only one country: Turkey .

Ideology & Tactics

The Syrian Democratic Forces is made up of several organizations that espouse a diverse variety of beliefs. The largest of which, the YPG , is a left wing Kurdish group considered the Syrian branch of the PKK and the militant branch of the PYD political party, a group that espouses a form of democratic federalism. While many groups are majority Kurdish, and others Arab, all are anti-islamist and all engage in combat operations as one against ISIS , al-Nusra, Turkish backed opposition groups, and others. The YPG’s alliance with Arab secular and moderate islamist groups during the height of the Siege of Kobani shows their willingness to forgo some of their principles in a time of need, where fighting evil is a paramount concern. However, in recent years, the Arab factions of the SDF drifted farther away from the Kurdish core, oftentimes revolting against al-Hasakah and aligning with whoever is leading the government in Damascus. One of the SDF's largest allies in the world is the often the dodgy and flaky United States , who despite their designation of the PKK as a terrorist group, continues to arm, train, and send vehicles to the SDF. Inside the YPG itself, one can find the YPJ, a female-led and female controlled branch that actively fights for secularism and free Kurdistan. Governance wise, the AANES is divided into many “cantons” which are divided further into 3000 communes. In the constitution of the AANES, the cantons are given self rule and ability to elect their own assemblies and representatives to the legislative assembly. Like the Republican faction during the Spanish Civil War, the ideals of Rojava attracted hundreds of left wing, anarchist, communist, and feminist foreign fighters from all over the world who utilized their experience to help the SDF wage war against IS and the Turks .

Tactically, like other MENA militant groups, the SDF uses truck technicals, often outfitted with heavy armor as a substitute for real armored vehicles and tanks. They also outfitted lighter armored vehicles with more plates to protect from IS AT. The few MBTs they owned in the earlier years were used far more sparingly and were modified with anti-missile tank cages. As cooperation with the international coalition increased, the SDF's access to American vehicles like the hummvee or the maxxpro also increased. Against the Turks and their groups in Syria, the SDF used nonconventional and guerilla strategies. They would use infiltration to gain intel and distraction, not too dissimilar to the inghisami tactics of groups like HTS . In the Afrin and Jarabulus region, YPG /J sleeper cells would launch attacks against the Turkish occupation, keeping them on edge.

Notable Actions

Fig 1. PRESS CONFERENCE BETWEEN REPRESENTATIVES OF DIFFERENT KURDISH AND ARAB SECULAR LEFT WING GROUPS ANNOUNCING THE CREATION OF THE SDF c. OCTOBER OF 2015

• October 10, 2015: The coalition of the Kurdish YPG and Arab opposition groups in North Eastern Syria that had fought in Kobani and Hasakah coalesced formally into the Syrian Democratic Forces. The groups within the newly formed SDF all had one common issue: their hatred of the Islamic State . Most groups were federalist and pro-democracy and some were arab tribal militias who have since had a mixed relationship with the rest of the group, especially the YPG .

• November 13, 2015: The SDF 's first large victory together was the capture of al-Hawl from Islamic State forces , as well as several gas stations, a dam, and a few oil fields. The fall of al-Hawl further secured the position of the largest city under their control: al-Hasakah.

• December 26, 2015: The SDF captured the strategic Tishrin Dam from Islamic State forces , opening up the way for further advances in the Aleppo countryside, specifically Manbij and al-Bab. Tishrin Dam was also one of three crossings across the Euphrates, a fact that unsettled the Turks , who moved into Aleppo countryside in 2016 during Operation Euphrates Shield.

• February 15, 2016: Amidst clashes between the opposition and the Regime north of Aleppo, the SDF launched an offensive to capture Tell Rifaat from nearby opposition islamist factions . Tell Rifaat would become a SDF stronghold, where the group's Arab factions would reside. A few years later, the fall of Afrin would result in the flow of refugees and militants into this small sliver of land.

• February 21, 2016: From Al-Hawl and al-Hasakah, the SDF attacked and captured the city of al-Shadadi, an important route between the capitals of the Islamic State in Syria and Iraq: Raqqa and Mosul. It was also a key training base and important jumping off point for an offensive against the Hasakah province. The fall of Shadadi ended its vast slave markets and also marked the full liberation of Hasakah province.

• August 12, 2016: The SDF secures the large regional center of Manbij in the Aleppo countryside. This development came after the the SDF launched a major regional offensive, encircling the city and putting it under siege until IS forces started to crack under pressure. Previously, Manbij was notorious for its role in smuggling IS fighters from the Turkish border and the city's fall marked the largest victory against the Islamic State up to that point since the end of Kobani. From Manbij, the SDF would launch incursions into the Jarabulus and al-Bab countryside. American and other coalition forces were also heavily involved in support roles.

• August 23, 2016: After a week of clashes, Baathist regime forces withdrew from bases within the regional Kurdish majority city of al-Hasakah, representing the first significant clash between the SDF and the regime during the civil war. Interestingly, the Americans helped establish a sort of "No Fly Zone" against incoming Regime jets.

• November 23, 2016: In the wake of the start of Turkey's Operation Euphrates Shield, an offensive to take the major town of al-Bab, the SDF moved to secure the vacuum once filled up by retreating Islamic State forces, before the TSK and their rebels could seize those towns and villages. The SDF took control of Arima from the east and Bayt Issa from Afrin in the west. By March of next year, Turkish forces were finally able to crack and capture al-Bab, suffering heavy losses, including 10 Leopard 2s.

Fig 2. SDF FIGHTER WATCHES OVER THE RUINS OF A STRATEGIC CITY SQUARE IN RAQQA FOLLOWING ITS LIBERATION c. OCTOBER OF 2017

• May 10, 2017: The SDF announced that both Tabqa city and its strategically significant dam were captured from Islamic State forces. The offensive started in February with US marines landing near Tabqa, allowing the SDF to go around Tabqa and besiege both the city and the dam. Though the campaign was slow and costly, the fall of Tabqa was a necessary stepping stone for the SDF before they could unleash hell upon Raqqa.

• October 20, 2017: The SDF declared that the entire city of Raqqah, the once great capital and stronghold for the Islamic State , had fallen after a four month tough campaign through the city that involved frequent coalition airstrikes and intense urban warfare. The SDF , including its Arab and Internationalist factions, was the main ground force fighting with the coalition, putting the city under siege through the north and the south, with the others mainly taking up an advisory or air support role. Up to 1500 civilians were killed, the vast majority of its population fled, and 4/5ths of the city was destroyed in the fighting. The cost was heavy, but the eventual outcome? A slow but undeniable down curve for the fates of the Islamic State . This, along with the fall of Mosul to Iraqi Security Forces , was the turning point of the conventional war.

• February 7, 2018: The Syrian Arab Army , Wagner, and a hodgepodge of pro-Regime forces launched an attack against a Syrian Democratic Forces base staffed with coalition forces near Assadist controlled Khasham. The resulting clash was a bloodbath for the Russo -Syrian force, resulting in anywhere between 55-200 dead and a dozen or so destroyed armored vehicles but no casualties whatsoever for the SDF -American force. From this point onward, Assadist attempts at controlling the other side of the Euphrates would be done in secrecy with infiltrations, not overt ground campaigns.

• March 18, 2018: Relations between the United States and Turkey sharply deteriorated when the former announced plans to train a strong force of 30,000 for the SDF along the border. Using this announcement as his justification, Erdogan executed Operation Olive Branch, an ironically named offensive geared at the capture of Afrin. It was never close. In a matter of eight weeks, Turkish forces captured and seized the entire Afrin region and pushing the SDF into insurgency and closer to the Assad regime. Trump's weak reaction to the battering of his ally further triggered riskier and more aggressive Turkish actions. Since then, the Turkish occupation of the Afrin region has been mired in allegations of ethnic cleansing.

• December 14, 2018: During the final stages of the conventional campaign against the Islamic State known as "Al-Jazeera Storm", the SDF captured the town of Hajin, the capital of the flailing and sordid Caliphate. Fighting incurred heavy casualties as coalition airstrikes hammered the Islamic State .

Fig 3. DESTRUCTION OF A TURKISH AFV/TANK IN TELL ABAYAD BY THE SDF c. OCTOBER OF 2019

• March 23, 2019: The SDF and coalition forces declared that the last pocket of Islamic State resistance in Syria, Baghuz Fawqani, had fallen. This declaration came after a month of brutal fighting with high civilian casualties in Baghuz's "tent city". Clashes still continued for days with coalition forces combing through the tent city's vast tunnel system. From now on, the war against the Islamic State would primarily be one of counterinsurgency against a prevalent, but low level threat.

• October 9, 2019: After a phone call between Erdogan and Trump resulted in the latter feeling comfortable enough to announce the withdrawal of US forces from the Syrian-Turkish border. Big mistake. Only 3 days later, the Turkish military launched large scale ground operations against SDF controlled Tell Abayad and Ras al-Ayn as an attempt to establish a "safe zone" across the border region to resettle Syrian refugees. In 6 weeks, Turkish and Opposition forces were able to secure a sizeable strip of territory, including cities and towns captured in offensives from ISIS 5 years ago. A ceasefire was eventually agreed to, but the effects were made visible. American allies got uneasier and contingents of Russians and Assadists were invited into Rojava.

• January 20, 2022: Heavy clashes, the worst since 2019, erupted after an attack on a prison in al-Hasakah freed hundreds of Islamic State fighters, including several high ranking officers. Islamic State attempts to break out into nearby districts of the city failed and, with the help of coalition airstrikes, they cleared the city. Up to 500 people were killed in total and two IS emirs were able to flee.

• August 27, 2023: Clashes broke out between the SDF and Arab tribesmen along the right, SDF half of the Euphrates River. This move has been the apparent result of increasing tensions within the Arab factions of the SDF and also increasing levels of operatives and planning by the Assad Regime against Rojava.

• December 3, 2024: During the rapid fall of the Assad Regime , the Syrian Democratic Forces pounced on the opportunity and seized both the Khasham pocket, the Al-Bukamal border crossing, and the city/regional capital of Deir Ez-Zor. The operation, supported by coalition aircaft, was nominally done in the name of filling in the power vacuum in the western euphrates before the Islamic State could seize the moment. In these newly captured towns and cities, the SDF was highly unpopular, leading to the group's transfer of seized territory on the right side of the Euphrates to the new HTS -led government.

• December 11, 2024: Taking advantage of the chaos of the previous week, the SNA launched a large ground offensive against the eight year SDF -held Manbij pocket. They took the main prize, the city, and advanced all the way up to the Qara-Qozak bridge and Tishrin Dam. There, fighting continued for months until a deal was made, officially transferring both to the Syrian Transitional Government . However, implementation was spotty and didn't really happen by January of 2026.

• March 10, 2025: In a landmark deal, the SDF agreed to merge its institutions and armed forces into the new Syrian state. Thus beginning the transitionary period. This ongoing period is marked by continued negotiations between the islamist government and the secular SDF /AANES. On one side, the SDF want a democratic and federal state, allowing for minorities to have a level of autonomy from Damascus . Meanwhile, the STG wants a strong, but unitary state; Damascus essentially rebuking this demand. From the sidelines, the Americans and Qataris help broker talks.

Fig 4. MEN RAISE THE FLAG OF THE NEW SYRIAN GOVERNMENT AFTER THE RAPID WITHDRAWAL FROM RAQQA c. JANUARY OF 2026

• January 16, 2026: Riding high from their victories during the fall of Sheikh Maqsoud in Aleppo, Syrian government forces pushed ahead after a negotiated settlement gave them Deir Hafer and Masakanah in Aleppo Governorate. Blaming the SDF for their failures in upholding the March 10 Agreement, the Syrian Army went beyond the deal and launched a major offensive against SDF territory on the western bank of the euphrates, taking the city of Tabqa. The uprising of the Arab tribes against the SDF and the entry of Syrian Army forces from multiple locations across the Euphrates resulted in a major collapse of the frontline. The SDF lost everything they had been building up for 8 years, including the city of Raqqa, in a matter of one or two days. However, the Syrian Government refrained from moving into Kobani, Hasakah, or Qamishili, preferring instead to move back to the negotiation table. Unlike with the March 10 agreement, major progress has been made in integration and Kurdish rights have been guaranteed by the Government .
