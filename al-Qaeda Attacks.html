Notable Attacks Involving al-Qaeda in the West

• February 27, 1993: Salafi jihadists launched their first attack on American soil when four militants carried out a bombing of one of the World Trade Center Towers in New York City, failing to make it collapse, but succeeding in killing six and injuring over 1,000. While they had no direct backing from al-Qaeda , the militants that had carried out the bombing were led by veterans of the USSR's war in Afghanistan and had familial ties to al-Qaeda . The attack on the WTC and continued violations by American supported states and groups led to Osama Bin Laden's fatwa in 1998, which commanded attacks against American civilians. It would lead to 9/11.

• September 11, 2001: In an attack colloquially known as 9/11, al-Qaeda terrorists hijacked four planes on the east coast, crashing two into the World Trade Center in New York City, one into a field in Pennsylvania initially targeting somewhere in Washington D.C., and the last one into the Pentagon in northern Virginia. By the end of the day, 2,977 people were killed, marking the deadliest attack in American , world, and al-Qaeda history. The attack has been planned, theorized, and justified through Bin Laden's Fatwas, or religious orders, for years, with most of the 19 hijackers having had extensive training in Afghanistan and had direct sponsorship from Osama Bin Laden to carry out the attacks. Bin Laden had been issuing fatwas on attacking American civilians on their territory until their total withdrawal from the Middle East and the revocation of relations with the state of Israel for years. The attack changed the psyche of the United States and shifted fear and hatred towards Muslims like never before. President Bush declared a "global war on terror" and invoked Article 5 of NATO , leading to an invasion of Taliban controlled Afghanistan , who had just refused the extradition of Osama Bin Laden and al-Qaeda terrorists. The failures of the United States during its counterinsurgency in Afghanistan and later in Iraq would lead to an explosion of terrorism and violence against America and its interests throughout the 2000s and 2010s. It can be reasonably said that 9/11 remains the most important event of the 21st century.

• January 14, 2015: Islamist militants shouting "we have avenged the prophet Muhammad" and the Takbir attacked the office of the Charlie Hebdo, a French satirical cartoon magazine, killing 12 and injuring 11, most were cartoonists and journalists, but some others were police officers, including one French-Muslim officer who died in the line of duty. The attack had occurred after the magazine had issued multiple racist and offensive cartoons depicting the Prophet Muhammad, angering many Muslims around the world, including extremists from al-Qaeda in the Arab Peninsula (AQAP) , who trained the terrorists involved in the attack.

• December 6, 2019: An Islamist extremist opened fire on American soldiers on a naval base in Penascola, Florida, killing 3 and injuring 8. The terrorist was actually a part of a Saudi task force that had been sent to Florida to participate in a training mission but attacked the Americans instead. A few months later,AQAP claimed responsibility, saying that the killer had been in contact with them and that the attack was done in revenge for American actions across the Muslim world. The FBI backed up the claim.

• March 11, 2004 and July 7, 2005: Two terrorist attacks occurred on 2004 in Spain and 2005 in the UK , killing 193 and 52 respectively. While these attacks were carried out by salafi jihadists with ties to al-Qaeda , prosecutors did not find a direct link to any al-Qaeda group.

Notable Attacks Involving the al-Nusra Front During the Syrian Civil War

• January 6, 2012: Jabhat al-Nusra , an al-Qaeda branch in Syria that had been created that month, claimed responsibility for a suicide bombing that targeted riot police in southern Damascus, killing 26 and injuring over 60. As major protests were still ongoing and the insurgency was still nascent, the bombing contributed to the acceleration of violence as it gave the regime even more justification to crack down even heavier.

• February 10, 2012: Jabhat al-Nusra commenced its campaign in the city of Aleppo when two suicide bombers detonated themselves near Syrian military and police buildings, killing 28, including 4 civilians, and injuring over 230. Syrian opposition groups like the FSA denounced the attack, saying that it may have been an Assadist false flag. However, al-Nusra eventually came out to claim responsibility for the attack, dispelling some rumors.

• March 17, 2012: Two al-Nusra car bombers detonated themselves in Assad regime bases in the city of Damascus, killing 27 and injuring 140. The al-Nusra Front later justified their attack was in response to the crimes of the regime , including in Homs, where the regime's siege had killed hundreds of civilians.

• February 21, 2013: During the rebel offensive on Damascus, jihadist militants detonated themselves near Baath party offices and the Russian embassy in Syria , killing 83 people, mostly civilians. It was one of the largest bombings conducted by al-Nusra during the Syrian civil war. Because of its high civilian toll, the group was a bit more hesitant to claim responsibility, despite claiming 7 other attacks in the month in the city of Damascus.

• July 22, 2013: After rebel forces fully wrestled control of Khan al-Assal from regime forces after months of heavy clashes and a regime chemical attack, the al-Nusra Front executed 51 SAA POWs in a brutal firing squad esque massacre. Meanwhile, the Syrian government had claimed that twice the amount of people had died in the massacre and that most of them were civilians.

• August 4, 2013: al-Nusra and then al-Qaeda aligned Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant participated in a rebel offensive attempting to make major ground in Latakia province, the stronghold of the Alawite community in Syria, the ethnic group the Assad dynasty belonged to. During the offensive, the al-Qaeda groups carried out dozens of sectarian killings, including against Alawite clerics and religious sites, as well as mass hostage taking. In the end, atleast 190 civilians were killed by opposition forces. Eventually, the Regime got the upperhand and pushed the rebels back to where they came from.

• December 12, 2013: During a rebel offensive in Eastern Ghouta's Damascus suburbs, Jaysh al-Islam and al-Nusra fighters entered the diverse town of Adra, killing up to or above 40 civilians from ethnic and religious minorities. The militants had reportedly gone door to door shooting people in their homes. al-Nusra and other salafi jihadists have many grievances with groups like the Alawites and the Druze, who in their eyes, are not just Kafirs, but also actively supporting the regime kill Muslims.

• June 10, 2015: When local Druze in the town of Qalb Loze resisted their arrest warrant, al-Nusra front jihadists opened fire on them and executed 24 other civilians, marking the largest attack on the Druze community in Syria so far in the war. al-Nusra had officially condemned the attack, saying that they would review the incident and arrest everyone involved. However, according to other accounts, the group had let the chief architect of the massacre, a Tunisian foreign fighter, get off easy.

• May 12, 2016: From besieged positions near northern Homs and southern Hama governorate al-Nusra and Ahrar al-Sham took the town of Zaraa in a suprise attack, killing up to 30 Assadist militiamen and 30 civilians, who were predominantly Alawite, coming from the same sect the ruling family came from.

Notable Attacks Involving al-Qaeda in South Asia (Including Central Command)

• September 9, 2001: al-Qaeda assassinated Ahmad Shah Massoud, the highest ranking militant commander of the Northern Alliance , a coalition that had been fighting the Taliban after they had entered Kabul during the mid 1990s. al-Qaeda militants, masquerading as journalists, invited Massoud to an interview in Northern Alliance territory , before detonating their vests and killing him. Literally two days later, al-Qaeda went on to carry out the deadliest terror attacks in world history in New York City. Many believe that Massoud's death led directly to the attack, as the anti-Taliban resistance would lack a key central leader.

• June 2, 2008: After an independent Danish magazine had published scandalous images of the Prophet Muhammad, an al-Qaeda militant detonated his SVBIED near the Danish embassy in Islamabad, Pakistan , killing six and injuring 24. al-Qaeda claimed responsibility and called on Muslims to harm Denmark wherever they could. As with other attacks following a westerner insulting the Prophet, the jihadist organization may have wanted to garner support from the Muslims in general, who were furious at the Danish cartoon.

• December 30, 2009: An al-Qaeda triple agent detonated himself in a CIA facility in FOB Chapman on the Afghan border with Pakistan , killing nine and injuring six, mostly CIA officers. The perpetrator, descendants of Palestinian refugees who were kicked out by Kuwait after the US intervention, while having a long history of extremism and creating propaganda, was believed to have crucial information and would be used to penetrate the organization from within.

• February 13, 2010: A bomb detonated in a German bakery near a Jewish center in Pune, central India , killing 18 and injuring over 50. While other groups under suspicion like Indian Mujahideen and Lashkar-e-Taiba refrained from claiming responsibility, an Egyptian al-Qaeda commander in the region did, saying the attack targeted the "foreign Israeli" jews. If true, this would be the group's largest attack in south Asia, where the group had slowly been recruiting locals.

Notable Attacks Involving al-Qaeda in the Horn of Africa (including al-Shabaab)

• August 7, 1998: In al-Qaeda's first attacks in east Africa and its worst attack before 9/11, two suicide bombers detonated themselves outside of US embassies in Nairobi, Kenya , and Dar es Salaam, Tanzania , killing 224, including 12 Americans , and injuring thousands. The explosion in Nairobi had caused the collapse of a nearby building populated by students and college staff. It was claimed by a front organization for al-Qaeda , who had prepared for months for this event. The attack drew American anger, leading to a lawsuit against Sudan , who was accused of supporting or atleast failing to crack down on al-Qaeda , and US navy strikes on alleged al-Qaeda bases in Afghanistan and Sudan . The campaign failed to materialize serious results and Osama Bin Laden would go back to planning.

• September 21, 2013: In retaliation for Kenya's intervention against the jihadist group in southern Somalia , al-Shabaab militants attacked a major shopping mall in Nairobi, the capital of Kenya , killing 62 civilians, injuring 160, and destroying the mall itself. They "vetted" the muslims from the non muslims by forcing everyone to say the Shahada. If you fail, you die. After the incident was resolved after days of clashes, a number of people heavily criticized the government's response, reportedly failing to stop it in time, despite getting a heads up, and failing to catch the militants. It was al-Shabaab's largest terrorist attack in Kenya so far in this war.

• June 15, 2014: Two successive attacks targeting the Christian town of Mpeketoni, Kenya and its satellite villages, killing around 70 people. Both attacks involved jihadists going door to door forcefully asking if people were Muslim, killing them if they said no. Despite al-Shabaab claiming responsibility for the second attack, some local officials said that it was perpetrated by "gangs" or people with local ethnic grievances. HRW later released a report alleging that security forces had committed abuses against ethnic Somalis in nearby towns in the name of finding the terrorists.

• April 2, 2015: Four al-Shabaab gunmen attacked Garissa University College in Kenya , killing 148 and injuring 79. The militants held hundreds hostage, identifying and selectively executing Christians. Eventually, Kenyan commandos came in and killed all four gunmen, who wore suicide vests. Al-Shabaab claimed responsibility, saying the attack was done because of Kenya's involvement with the African Union mission in Somalia . The attack remains the deadliest in the history of Kenya during the 21st century. It was later revealed that multiple people tried to warn Kenyan authorities of an impending disaster at the university, but were left on deaf ears.

• October 14, 2017: An SVBIED detonated itself in a busy location near a major hotel and the Qatari embassy in Somalia , its effects compounded by its causing of a major explosion at a nearby fuel tanker. The blast caused the hotel to collapse and heavily damaged the embassy. A smaller suicide bombing occurred 30 minutes after the first, killing only a few. In total, 587 were killed and 316 were injured. It was later revealed that the bomber had actually intended to detonate himself in the airport compound, but was forced to stop after nearby SNA troops had flattened his tire as he was speeding by. Due to its sheer scale and the massive number of total death, no group claimed responsibility, but al-Shabaab is widely recognized to be the only force willing to carry out such an attack. Maybe claiming responsibility would've painted a massive target on its back and angered millions of Somalis who may have been sympathetic. Meanwhile, This attack became one of the deadliest terrorist attacks in world history and the deadliest in Africa's history. Especially notable as all of its damage primarily came from a single suicide bomber.

• January 15, 2019: Four al-Shabaab gunmen and one suicide bomber attacked an office and hotel complex in Kenya's capital city in Nairobi hosting numerous international companies, killing 22 civilians and wounding 27. Kenyan forces, commanded by a British officer in the country for training, cleared the building after nearly a day of clashes. The attack was soon claimed by al-Shabaab who said it was done in response to America's recognition of Jerusalem as capital of Israel , an extremely controversial move in the west and especially in the Muslim world.

• December 28, 2019: An SVBIED was detonated in a police checkpoint in Somalia's capital, Mogadishu, during busy rush hour, killing 85 and injuring more than 140, including anyone from university students to Turkish engineers. Al-Shabaab claimed responsibility, saying it was in revenge for Turkish involvement in the country's civil war.

• October 29, 2022: Weeks after the Somalia's military announced the start of offensive operations in the center of the country, two al-Shabaab car bombs detonated inside of Mogadishu near the ministry of education, killing more than 120 and injuring atleast 300, resulting in the deadliest attack by the terrorist group in five years. The attack strengthened the resolve of the SNA during the offensive, but not enough to seriously crack or destroy the group in Shabelle and South West.

• August 2, 2024: Multiple al-Shabaab jihadists launched a terrorist attack on the Lido Beach in Mogadishu, Somalia involving small arms and suicide bombs, killing over 50 and injuring over 200. The attack was the deadliest in Somalia for two years. The beach was very popular with locals and government officials, so attacking it showed the vulnerabilities of the capital city. A few months later, al-Shabaab launched a major nationwide offensive, seizing major parts of provinces around the capital.

Notable Attacks Involving al-Qaeda groups in Iraq

• February 1, 2004: In the opening phase of the insurgency against the US occupation , two jihadist suicide bombers detonated themselves in Erbil in Iraq's Kurdish autonomous region near Kurdish political party offices during Eid al-Adha festivites, killing 117 and injuring over 130, including local officials. The attack may have been retaliation for the Kurdish region's support for the US invasion , or for their role in the counterinsurgency campaign led by the US. While no group claimed responsibility, al-Qaeda and a local Kurdish jihadist group were suspects. If conducted by the former, it would be one of al-Qaeda 's first terrorist attacks in Iraq .

• February 28, 2005: A Jordanian al-Qaeda member detonated itself near a recruiting center for Iraq 's police forces in al-Hilla, Iraq , killing 127. The bomber was noted to have come from an upper middle class family, with history in the United States . He was celebrated among jihadist sites and even his family, causing controversy in Jordan .

• April 12, 2007: For the first time since the invasion, Islamic State of Iraq al-Qaeda in Iraq claimed a suicide bombing at Iraq's parliament building, killing up to eight, including three MPs, and injured more than 20. The attack had occurred in the heavily fortified Green Zone, which was supposed to thwart such an attack. While the attack may not have killed many people, it showed the weakness of the central government during the time of the sectarian civil war.

• August 14, 2007: During the escalating and brutal sectarian civil war in Iraq , four SVBIEDs detonated in two Yazidi towns in northern Iraq , killing 796 and injuring over 1,500, marking the largest and most deadly suicide bombing during the Iraq war and one of the deadliest terrorist attacks in modern history. The large blasts from the bombings had crumbled many buildings, trapping families and giving first responders a hard time. The hospitals themselves were horrifically overstretched and waning on necessary equipment. While no group had claimed responsibility, Islamic State in Iraq was suspected to have carried out the attack. Salafi jihadists like ISI see Yazidis, a Kurdish speaking ethnoreligious group, as "anti-Islamic" infidels. It was one of the deadliest sectarian attacks against the Yazidis before an independent ISIS would come in three quarters of a decade later and launch their own genocide.

• August 19, 2009: Three suicide bombers detonated themselves in multiple Iraqi government ministiries, including finance, foreign affairs, a security patrol, and a hotel in Baghdad, killing 101 and injuring over 500. The Islamic State in Iraq claimed these attacks. It was the first large attack of the year.

• October 25, 2009: Two Islamic State in Iraq suicide bombers detonated themselves inside of a minivan and bus near the offices of the ministry of justice and the municipal council, killing 155 and injuring over 700. Both buildings and a church suffered major damage, killing dozens of government employees. It came during a period of relative quiet during the insurgency, with PM Maliki presenting himself as a reliable and strong leader during the run up to parliamentary elections. Some whispered if this could delay the US withdrawal from Iraq .

• December 8, 2009: In the trailing end of the US war in Iraq , ISI militants detonated their SVBIEDs in Baghdad, targeting government and security buildings, killing over 120 people and injuring several hundred. As always, Iraqi security forces were blamed for their inadequacy and failure to stem or prevent the attack.

Notable Attacks Involving al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb (AQIM)

• April 11, 2002: A jihadist SVBIED detonated at the historic Ghriba synagogue in Tunisia , killing 19 and injuring over 30. Of the 19, the vast majority were tourists from Germany , with some Tunisians and Frenchmen . al-Qaeda claimed responsibility for the atrocity, citing revenge for the deeds committed against the Palestinians by the Israelis . The main room of the synagogue, a site that had been there in one shape or an another for centuries, was heavily damaged.

• May 16, 2003: Twelve Jihadist suicide bombers detonated themselves next to several Jewish sites in Casablanca, Morocco , killing 33, including some nationals from western Europe, and injuring a hundred. The jihadists, who had planned the attack for months, with some even fighting in Afghanistan , had failed to consider that the Jews were home observing Shabbat. If they had, the attack may have been much deadlier. al-Qaeda claimed responsibility for the attacks and an affiliate of the group was heavily suspected in leading the ordeal. In the aftermath, the Moroccan state tightened restrictions and laws surrounding Islamism.

• April 11, 2007: Two SVBIEDs detonated in Algiers, the capital of Algeria , targeting the office of the Algerian PM and a police station, killing 33 and injuring over 150. al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb claimed responsibility for both attacks. They even said that the office was Algeria's "World Trade Center", an obvious allusion to the towers in New York that were taken down on 9/11.

• December 11, 2007: Two SVBIEDs detonated outside of UNHCR offices and the supreme constitutional court of Algeria , killing 41, including 17 UN employees, and injuring 170. The bombing created extensive damage to the UN building. The attack was claimed by al-Qaeda .

• April 19, 2008: An SVBIED detonated next to paramilitary recruits waiting to take entry exams in Issers, northern Algeria , killing 43 and injuring 38. Algeria officials considered it the deadliest terrorist attack in years.

• January 16, 2013: 29 Jihadists violently stormed a natural gas plant in In Amenas, southeastern Algeria , taking 800 hostage, including 132 foreign nationals. The militants demanded the end of French intervention against them in northern Mali . The crisis had dragged on for three days, before Algeria special forces launched a major rescue operation, freeing the 800 and killing all of the militants. In the end, 39 foreign workers and one security guard was killed. The attack was carried out by al-Mourabitoun, an arm of al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb . Later analysis revealed severe lapses in security by the government and the gas companies, even though the region was already visibly worsening.

Notable Attacks Involving Jamaat Nasr al-Islam wal-Muslimeen (JNIM), Mourabitoun, and other al-Qaeda in the Sahel

• January 15, 2016: Half a dozen militants stormed a restaurant and hotel in the Burkinabe capital, taking more than a 170 hostages. Eventually, Burkinabe commandos came by the next time, clashing with the militants violently before killing them and freeing the hostages. In total, 30 people were killed, with a majority from Europe or North America, and 56 were wounded. Some survivors saw the attackers specifically targeting "white" people. It was the deadliest attack committed by al-Qaeda in Burkina Faso thus far, reflecting the country's worsening security landscape since a coup rocked its streets only months before.

• September 3, 2019: A mine planted by JNIM in Mopti, central Mali , detonated as a civilian passenger bus ran over it, killing 14 and wounding 24. Bizzarely, this was one of the few times any al-Qaeda affiliate has apologized for any action. To the grieving families, JNIM offered their condolonces, said that the matter would be investigated with the values of Shariah, and explained that the mine was intended for nearby French patrols.

• June 4, 2021: Jihadists targeted the town of Solhan in northeast Burkina Faso , attacking its military base and going door to door and executing civilians, killing around 160 people. This attack marked the deadliest in Burkinabe history up to that point. JNIM officially condemned and rejected the attack. Interestingly, the Islamic State condemned it as well and blamed it on JNIM, a conclusion that the Burkinabe government also came to during their own investigations. The government also claimed that the attack was done for looting and financing the group's activities, while others claimed revenge or pressure. The province is majority Fulani, which matches with most of JNIM .

• May 25, 2022: Refugees running from the siege of Madjoari were intercepted and executed by JNIM militants near the town of Singou in Burkina Faso's southeast, resulting in atleast 50 deaths and a similar amount were missing. All of the dead, according to the governor of the region, were military aged men, who were singled out from the women and the children. The attack was done in revenge for a failed JNIM assault on Madjoari's military that resulted in heavy casualties.

• September 7, 2023: Jihadists attacked the Tomboctou, a civilian boat controlled by a Moroccan company going from Mopti to Gao along the Niger river in Mali , with rockets and small arms fire, resulting in its sinking and the deaths of atleast 154, marking one of the deadliest al-Qaeda terrorist attacks in the country. The group may have targeted the ship in order to establish their dominance over the important transport and trade routes on the Niger river, where roads are lacking. JNIM claimed responsibility for the attack on the boat. Simultaneously, JNIM militants attacked an army base in Gao, not much farther than the boat sinking. That attack killed 15 soldiers.

• August 24, 2024: JNIM militants attacked the town of Barsalogho in central Burkina Faso , killing atleast 200 civilians, who were only guarded by a dozen Burkinabe soldiers as they were forced to build a circle of trenches around the town for the military. JNIM proudly claimed responsibility, saying this attack was in response for a government massacre in the same province that killed atleast 200 ethnically Fulani civilians. The government response and their failure to protect hundreds of civilians laboring in a tense area was heavily critized by international observers and locals alike. Fulanis are a large majority Muslim ethnic group that makes up much of JNIM and other jihadist groups for several reasons, including poverty and discrimination. The massacre was the worst in the Sahel region carried out by al-Qaeda forces.

Other Notable al-Qaeda Attacks

• December 29, 1992: Two Afghan war veterans planted bombs in two Yemeni hotels in the major port city of Aden, killing two and injuring five. The bombing attempted to kill US soldiers who were setting up their logistics for a combat operation in Somalia , however, no Marines were actually killed as the detonations were poorly executed. Years later, Osama Bin Laden took credit for the bombings in Yemen , marking their first militant action outside of Afghanistan. Despite no American deaths, they would withdraw from Yemen anyway, giving Bin Laden a propaganda victory, their first against the US .

• October 12, 2000: The USS Cole, docked in the Yemeni port of Aden, was targeted in a suicide bombing by al-Qaeda terrorists using a small boat laden with explosives, killing 15. The USS Cole bombing was allegedly done to provoke the Americans into launching a military campaign, but it had failed.

• October 12, 2002: Two suicide bombers detonated themselves in two different night clubs in the hotspot of Bali, Indonesia , a city known for hosting Australian tourists, during busy season. The attack was a "double tap", since the second bomber detonated itself after party-goers had left the first nightclub aftert that detonation. In the end, 202 people had died, 88 Australian nationals, and 209 were wounded. Jemeaah Islamiyah , a group with many ties to al-Qaeda , is believed to have carried out the attack. However, al-Qaeda's Osama Bin Laden later reportedly claimed responsibility on tape, saying it was a retaliation against Australia's involvement in Afghanistan and Indonesia . It was later revealed in an investigationt that al-Qaeda had directly funded the attack. Maybe al-Qaeda 's lack of reach in the region was solved by working with JI. The attack was the deadliest in Indonesia and the deadliest against Australians .

• May 12, 2003: A group of jihadist militants unloaded themselves next to hotels in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia , that were frequented by foreigners, including Americans , and stormed three hotels, opening fire on anyone and detonating explosives. In the end, 27 people were killed, the majority of which were foreigners, and over 160 people were injured. A few months later, another attack occurred near a hotel in Riyadh, killing 17. al-Qaeda claimed the attack straight from Afghanistan through their new and innovative online media wing. Some hawks among the gulf even claimed that the Iranians had "ordered the attack", as the planner, Sayf al-Adel, is believed to be in Iran .

• November 15, 2003: Four jihadist suicide bombers detonated themselves in four locations in Istanbul, the largest city in Turkey , including two synagogues, and a few days later, a British consulate, and a bank, killing 55 and injuring 750. While a militant Turkish nationalist group had initially claimed responsibility, Turkish investigators eventually claimed that the attack was carried out and planned by al-Qaeda members and veterans. The attack was the deadliest attack carried out yet on Turkish soil and inspired new government counterterror operations against Islamists in that country.

• November 9, 2005: In response to Jordan's support for the United States and Israel , three Jihadist militants detonated their suicide vests at three separate hotels in Amman, the capital of Jordan , killing 57, including numerous foreign citizens, and wounding 115. The attack was claimed by al-Qaeda's leader, Abu Musab al-Zarqawi pointed out the country's purported zionism and its use as a gateway for Americans and their officials going into Iraq . Zionism or not, one of the targets was actually hosting a Palestinian wedding, as Jordan had taken in hundreds of thousands of Palestinian refugees .
