Hezbollah

Updated: 2/9/25

Overview

Hezbollah is a Lebanese shi'ite islamist political group and militant organization. Hezbollah arose as an extremist counterweight to both entrenched Amal interests and the Israeli occupation of southern Lebanon , espousing a form of shi'ite nationalism made popular after the 1979 Iranian revolution. Hezbollah waged guerilla warfare and bombing raids against Israeli and American targets, eventually emerging victorious when the IDF withdrew from the country in 2000. The group would grow legitimacy politically and militarily, participating in Lebanese elections and practically de-facto controlling much of the south and the Beqaa valley. The militant group would grow its political influence in the region, waging a war with Israel in 2006 and fighting with the Assad Regime in the brutal conflict in Syria . Geopolitically, Hezbollah was one of the strongest and largest Iranian proxies in the region, hosting a vast arsenal of rockets and missiles. That was until 2024, when Hezbollah launched attacks in support of Hamas in Gaza, eventually resulting in an invasion of Lebanon and a decapitation of their leadership & armed capabilities. The weakening of Hezbollah has caused a major power vacuum in Lebanon and the region as a whole. Hezbollah has been listed as a terror group by the European Union, the US , the UK, Israel , the GCC, and France .

Ideology & Tactics

The beliefs of Hezbollah have been articulated in two manifestos, one written in 1985 and another in 2009. In its older 1985 manifesto, Hezbollah commended the Iranian revolution as a beacon of Islam against both west and east, condemned the United States as an enemy of Islam, vowed to fight Israel and liberate Palestinian territories, supported Shariah in Lebanon , and stated the group's refusal to work diplomatically within Lebanese government structure. They also stated that Christians should open their heart and join Islam. The open letter was created during a time when Hezbollah was still a new shi'ite revolutionary guerilla organization, lacking political power in comparison to its direct rival: Amal . Over the 1990s and 2000s, Hezbollah would drastically change as a group. The group's acceptance and joining of Lebanese politics, its growth as a conventional military threat, its de-facto governance of Southern Lebanon , and its two wars (one long, one short) with Israel had shifted the outlook of the group, requiring a new manifesto to reflect the new changes. In the new 2009 manifesto, Hezbollah reiterated their commitment to the anti-zionist, anti-American "resistance" headed by Iran in support of the "deoccupation" of Palestine . However, Hezbollah dropped mentions of implementing shariah or shi'ite dominance in Lebanon and vocally condemning sectarianism. The manifesto also maintains that Hezbollah will not disarm, despite calls from some in Lebanon and most in the international community. While the 2009 document showed how far the group had progressed from the 1980s, it does not reflect totally on the current status of the group. Since the manifesto's writing, Hezbollah joined the conflict in Syria in support of Bashar al-Assad, shielding and protecting the holy Sayyida Zaynab shrine from jihadist rebels. The Zaynab shrine was the target of many such rebels including VBIEDs from the Islamic State .

During the Israeli occupation of Southern Lebanon , Hezbollah grew its members, plotting suicide bombings, rocket attacks, kidnappings, ambushes, and other guerilla war tactics. These high profile moves were done to put Hezbollah on the map and raise awareness for the group. Since the group was not in control of any territory during its insurgency phase, they primarily gained funding through a mix of Iranian and a variety of unconventional places, such as front organizations like charities and criminal networks throughout the region and places that host large Lebanese diaspora like Latin America. During the latter phase of the insurgency, however, Hezbollah began to grow stronger and more able to wage a sustained war of attrition against the IDF , eventually leading to Israel 's withdrawal from Southern Lebanon due to poor morale/political issues and the collapse of the SLA . In the first half of the 2000s, Hezbollah would continue to build up its military strength and rocketry. Hezbollah’s funding from Iran would strengthen following the fall of Saddam Hussein in Iraq and the destabilization of that country. These increased capabilities would bear fruit in 2006, when Hezbollah launched a cross border incursion, employing some of their earlier insurgency tactics, kidnapping some IDF soldiers. In the ensuing war with Israel , Hezbollah launched several missile and rocket attacks utilizing their vast tunnel network and conducting a defensive war. The group bombarded IDF ships and were able to hit cities deep within Israel like Haifa with their missiles and rockets supplied from Iran . Hezbollah employed Katyusha rockets from the second world war in order to attack quickly and quickly evade Israeli detection. These tactics made life for Israeli soldiers very hard, forcing them to withdraw in only a month. Over 120 IDF soldiers were killed, making it one of the shortest, but bloodiest, conflicts Israel has fought in. Hezbollah’s resistance against Israel in 2006 temporarily made the organization very well liked in the rest of the Arab World, atleast that was before their intervention in Syria . During the Syrian Civil War, Hezbollah supported the Assad regime in a conventional context. As stated before, they positioned themselves as defenders of Syrian shi’ites against terrorism and were able to train the army of Assad using their past experiences in asymmetrical conflict. The military capabilities of Hezbollah during the 2023-2024 conflict were vastly overestimated by many outside observers. Using lessons they had learned during its initial invasion of Gaza, Israel would wage war in a methodical fashion, destroying military infrastructure and launching large scale airstrikes on Hezbollah installations across the country. Hezbollah was unable to switch towards successful guerilla warfare against Israel and was forced to sign a ceasefire, in a far worse state than only a few years ago.

Notable Actions

• November 11, 1982: A Hezbollah SVBIED blew up while ramming into an IDF military command centre in Tyre, Southern Lebanon , killing 89-102 people, including 75 Israelis . This day remains one of the bloodiest single day events for Israeli forces at that time. A year later, another Hezbollah SVBIED blew up at a Shin Bet building in Tyre, killing 28 Israelis and 32 Lebanese Arabs. Perhaps due to the major Israeli loss, it took until 2024 for Israel to admit that the explosion was caused by a suicide bombing and not a gas cylinder explosion. This attack was concurrent with the then ongoing Israeli invasion of Lebanon .

Fig 1. AFTERMATH OF A HEZBOLLAH SUICIDE BOMBING TARGETING A US MARINE BARRACKS IN BEIRUT, LEBANON, DURING THEIR PEACEKEEPING c. OCTOBER OF 1983

• April 18, 1983: A Hezbollah SVBIED struck and exploded next to the American embassy in Beirut, killing 62 and destroying much of the building. More than half of the people killed in the bombing were Lebanese employees or visitors, not really helping Hezbollah's cause here. President Reagan denounced and condemned the "cowardly" act, with his congress ratcheting up economic and military aid for the country of Lebanon . Despite Hezbollah and Iran denying responsibility for the attack for years, a court in Washington D.C. stated that they had indeed committed the attack with Iranian glee and support.

• October 23, 1983: A series of suicide bombings rocked through Two French and American military bases in the city of Beirut, killing over 300. This large scale suicide attack, perhaps the largest prior to 9/11, was done by a predecessor of Hezbollah in Lebanon in retaliation for America and France's support for Israel in their ongoing invasion as well as their tacit support for Iraq during their invasion of Iran . This attack was the result of the combination of a highly dynamic and multi faceted middle east. The single deadliest day for American forces would result in the Americans withdrawing its marines from Lebanon and the start of a short bombardment of Syrian army positions in Beqaa.

• September 20, 1984: Hezbollah , with the backing of the Iranian IRGC, exploded an SVBIED once again near the recently moved American embassy in Beirut, marking the second time a US embassy in Beirut was targeted. In this attack, which was thoroughly practiced in Baalbek, over 22 people were killed and 90 were injured. This attack only killed two Americans , with the rest being local Lebanese (most likely christian). At this point, when Hezbollah remained a fringe group, it didn't really need to care what type of Lebanese they killed, as long as it wasn't part of their possible support base.

• December 25, 1986: Hezbollah operatives hijacked Iraqi Airways Flight 163 enroute from Saddam International Airport to Jordan . The four hijackers chucked hand grenades into the airplane, forcing the plane to attempt an emergency landing. Unfortunately for everyone onboard, a grenade exploded in the cockpit, causing the plane to crash in Saudi Arabia . 63 people were killed and 35 injured, marking one of the deadliest hijacking incidents in history.

Fig 1. HEZBOLLAH MILITANTS SETTING UP ROADBLOCKS AND CHECKPOINTS IN SOUTHERN LEBANON DURING THE WAR WITH AMAL c. MAY OF 1989

• April 5, 1988: Hezbollah , a relatively new and fresh political bloc for extremist shias, effectively went to war with the establishment, relatively moderate, and Syrian backed Amal Movement. The conflict started when Iranian backed Hezbollah attacked a convoy that included an Amal officer and an American official, capturing the later. Amal responded by waging war against Hezbollah , engaging in heavy clashes across shia towns in Southern Lebanon , the Baalbek valley, and in Southern Beirut. Ceasefires and agreements were brokered and quickly broken until both sides agreed to a Syrian brokered ceasefire. In the less than two years of fighting, 500-750 people were killed, while a few thousand more were wounded. Since this clash, Hezbollah has effectively become the face for the Shia Muslims in Southern Lebanon , usurping Amal .

• March 17, 1992: A Hezbollah suicide bomber driving a pickup truck rammed into the Israeli embassy in Buenos Aires, Argentina , and detonated the SVBIED. The attack killed 29 people and wounded over 240 others. This attack was Hezbollah's first in South America, but not their last nor their deadliest. In the attack only four Israelis were killed, while the vast majority were Argentinean civilians. After years of investigation, both Iran and numerous high ranking Hezbollah officials were convicted. But since these planners were hundreds of miles away in a different continent, there was no justice.

• September 30, 1992: Hezbollah attacked a position held by the IDF supporting South Lebanon Army near the city of Tyre. Clashes would kill 9, including a UN peacekeeper that was shot by Hezbollah when they refused to let their fighters by a checkpoint.

• July 25, 1993: The IDF launched Operation Accountability following intense clashes between Hezbollah and the Israeli military, killing many military personnel on many sides. The IDF struck the group directly, pounding Southern Lebanon with artillery, choppers, and jets, while Hezbollah fired 150 rockets into the Gallile. After 7 days, 140 Lebanese civilian dead, 13 Hezbollah dead, and one IDF member dead, a ceasefire was brokered by the United States by both parties. Though violations of the ceasefire were extremely prevalent in the days and even weeks after the conflict ended.

• July 18, 1994: An operative working with a group affiliated with Hezbollah launched a suicide bombing of the AMIA jewish community center in Buenos Aires, Argentina , killing 85 people and injuring over 300. This was the largest Hezbollah suicide bombing outside of Lebanon and one of the largest terrorist attacks on Argentina . In the 30 years following the attack, various different Argentinean governments underwent an investigation sometimes including Iranian and American cooperation. In April of 2024, Argentina 's court ruled that Iran was responsible and aimed to push the then government back into a nuclear cooperation agreement.

• April 11, 1996: After Hezbollah launched a rocket attack on northern Israel , alleging that the IDF killed a teenage boy through a roadside bomb, Israel launched Operation Grapes of Wrath as a retaliation. During that operation, the IDF struck many Hezbollah targets across Southern Lebanon , even some Syrian military elements, including anti-aircraft units. In response, Hezbollah launched more than one thousand Katyusha rockets, wounding 62 Israelis . 16 days after the operation started, a ceasefire was reached between both sides brokered by the US . In the end, 350,000-500,000 people were displaced, 149-250 civilians were killed, and 13 fighters were killed. Much of the civilian casualties were caused by an IDF artillery attack on a UN compound in Qana.

• September 4, 1997: Hezbollah forces pulled off a major ambush on elite IDF naval commandos, killing twelve in the Southern Lebanese town of Ansariya. To this point, this was the largest mass casualty event for the IDF during their occupation of Southern Lebanon . Many years later in 2010, Nasrallah revealed that they had downloaded videos taken by IDF drones and were able to pinpoint a possible IDF operation near the town.

Fig 1. HEZBOLLAH FIGHTERS PARADING THROUGH KFAR KILA WITH A CAPTURED T-55 MBT FROM THE SLA FOLLOWING ISRAELI WITHDRAWAL c. MAY OF 2000

• May 24, 2000: During the Israeli Withdrawal from Southern Lebanon , Hezbollah launched a major offensive to seize the entire south from Israel 's collaborator paramilitary, the South Lebanon Army (SLA) . The SLA collapsed at a rate faster than even Assad or the ANA , leaving the entire south under Hezbollah hands after only a few days with minimal clashes. Hezbollah swarmed through once SLA held towns, liberating brutal and oppressive prisons as SLA fighters withdrew into Israel . The 15 years or so of Israel 's attempted counterinsurgency were thrown in a matter of a few days. For the first time, Hezbollah would get direct access to Israel across their new, tense border.

• October 7, 2000: Only five months after Israeli forces withdrew from Southern Lebanon , Hezbollah launched a raid into the Shebaa Farms area, capturing three Israeli soldiers in an attempt to force a prisoner exchange and free some of Hezbollah 's captured in Israel . Four years later in 2004, Hezbollah and Israel agreed to a prisoner swap, exchanging the now dead bodies of the three IDF soldiers for 435 Lebanese and Palestinian prisoners . This was the first action by Hezbollah that was inside of Israel itself.

• February 14, 2005: Former Prime Minister of Lebanon and anti-Syrian politican Rafic Hariri was killed in a car bombing along with 21 others in Beirut. The assassination of Hariri triggered the Cedar Revolution, a massive wave of protests and unrests across the country that led to the withdrawal of all Syrian forces. Hezbollah , a major ally of Syria , was highly suspected to have launched this attack, even as they attempted to sloppily blame a fictional jihadist group. The investigation took years, but eventually multiple Hezbollah senior officials were tried and convicted in absentia. To this day, none of the members were actually arrested in connection with the bombing, but multiple were killed by Israel over the years.

Fig 2. HEZBOLLAH LAUNCHES KATYUSHA MLRS ROCKETS AT ISRAEL FROM SOUTHERN LEBANON c. JULY OF 2006

• November 21, 2005: In an attempt to perhaps stir up the same war that erupted only a year later, Hezbollah operatives crossed the border into Israel and attempted to kidnap Israeli soldiers stationed in the Shebaa Farms disputed zone. Despite using new types of missiles, rockets, and tactics, the attempt still failed and 4 fighters were killed.

• July 12, 2006: The largest armed conflict between Israel and Hezbollah erupts after the latter launched an attack across the border, killing eight IDF soldiers, capturing two, and escalating an ongoing dispute over Shebaa farms. Israel responds in full force, launching a full scale air campaign throughout the entirety of Lebanon and putting that country under a air and sea blockade. The Israelis also launched a limited ground invasion, attempting to disarm their positions across the border. In response, Hezbollah launched intense salvos of rockets and missiles into Northern Israel , four thousand in total, and engaged with guerilla warfare against the IDF . Hezbollah by this point was a highly trained, tactical, and sophisticated militant group, surprising many within the IDF . After 34 days of heated conflict, both sides agreed to a UNSC ceasefire and the war had ended. The war had weakened Hezbollah , but it had also shown Israelis that the group was far stronger than first thought. Israel 's failure to disarm Hezbollah had given a new sense of confidence for the group and its supporter in Tehran. For a time (atleast before the group's intervention in Syria ), Hezbollah was widely celebrated within the Arab and muslim world, even sunnis.

• May 7, 2008: Intense clashes erupted across Lebanon between Hezbollah and their allies against the ruling government of Lebanon , which was under the anti-Syrian pro-Cedar Revolution campaign. The bloodiest sectarian conflict since the Lebanese Civil War was triggered after the then Lebanese government attempted to crack down on the telecommunications network of Hezbollah 's armed wing. After 7 days and the deaths of 110 people, both sides signed the Doha Agreement of 2008, which included many concessions to Hezbollah . For the time being, the Lebanese state would leave Hezbollah alone, allowing it to continue to grow and arm itself.

Fig 3. HEZBOLLAH MILITANTS CLEARING BUILDINGS IN AL-QUSAYR OF SYRIAN OPPOSITION FIGHTERS c. MAY OF 2013

• June 6, 2013: Hezbollah assisted in the brutal siege and capture of al-Qusayr from Opposition forces, an important city on the northern half of the border between Lebanon and Syria . During the siege, Hezbollah fighters, who were more disciplined and trained than the SAA , led the spearhead of the offensive and moved in also from Beqaa Valley in Lebanon . The people of city went without water or medicine for more than two weeks and the fighters for Hezbollah were reported to have committed grave atrocities. Nevertheless, Al-Qusayr was a key route of supply to the contested city of Homs and its fall was considered a turning point of the war at the time as Hezbollah forces would be able to flow in and intervene in the battle for Homs.

• March 17, 2014: Thousands of Hezbollah fighters supported the Assad Regime's push to the Qalamoun mountains, seizing the important opposition stronghold of Yabroud, which operated as an important supply point for rebel -held Arsal, and eventually pushing up towards the Lebanese border. A few months later, the Lebanese Army launched their own offensive and seized Arsal, the largest town in Lebanon under rebel /jihadist hands, pushing them back farther into the fringes. The eventual fall of rebel strongholds across the Lebanese border in 2017 allowed for Hezbollah to move in more troops and heavy equipment to support Assad.

• January 28, 2015: Ten days after Israeli forces struck a convoy in Syria that killed six Hezbollah fighters and an IRGC brigadier general, Hezbollah launched a retaliatory attack in Shebaa Farms against a convoy of the IDF 's Givati Brigade, killing two and destroying two humvees. In retaliation, the IDF fired their artillery, resulting in a tense crisis. But neither side sought further escalation or blood letting.

• March 26, 2015: A Hezbollah field commander was killed during Jaysh al-Fatah's offensive to take control of Idlib, a major provincial city in Syria 's north. Due to Hezbollah 's concurrent planning for a Qalamoun offensive, they were unable to keep JaF from seizing it, resulting in the largest rebel victory since the fall of Raqqa in 2013.

• September 25, 2015: Hezbollah /SAA forces agree to a "refugee swap" and temporary ceasefire with opposition groups, allowing civilians and rebel militants to withdraw from the embattled and besieged rebel stronghold of Zabadani in the western Damascus countryside as well as allowing pro-government forces and civilians to withdraw from al-Fu'ah and Kafriya in Idlib governorate. Prior to the agreement, Hezbollah forces faced more than a hundred dead after more than two months of slow, but brutal fighting in Zabadani and nearby Madaya. All rebel militants were evacuated from Zabadani and Madaya by April of 2017.

Fig 4. HEZBOLLAH AND FIGHTERS FROM IRAQ INTERVIEWED BY PROPAGANDISTS OUTSIDE OF ALEPPO CITY c. SEPTEMBER OF 2016

• December 22, 2016: Assad's forces cleared and fully captured the major city of Aleppo from the myriad of opposition and rebel groups previously stationed there. They were able to do this with the support of various state and non state actors, including Hezbollah and various of their affiliates based in Syria . Hezbollah was deeply involved in advising and carrying out operations in the north in Nubl and Zahra and later by encircling and eventually capturing rebel held east Aleppo. While the civilian toll was high, analysts of the time believed that the fall of Aleppo represented a turning point even larger than that of the fall of Homs or al-Qusayr. A stronghold of the revolution had fallen.

• February 5, 2017: During the Turkish military's offensive on al-Bab in the Aleppo countryside against the Islamic State , the SAA used the opportunity to cut off IS forces in the Aleppo governorate by advancing all the way up to the shores of Lake Assad. Hezbollah , who had just recently been involved in the nearby action inside of the city of Aleppo, sent elite forces and assisted the regime.

• March 2, 2017: After approximately two years of intense fighting and tit for tat offensives, the Syrian army, in conjunction with Hezbollah and the Russians , finally achieved a breakthrough and seized the highly strategically and historically important desert city of Palmyra. Their contribution [Hezbollah 's], was sending their elite Redwan force in support of their Assadist allies. This victory, hard fought and concurrent to the much larger SDF offensive on Raqqa, enabled Government forces to slowly sweep through the Syrian desert. Meanwhile, Palmyra was heavily damaged and many parts of the historic and ancient city were destroyed by IS .

• October 25, 2017: Hundreds of Hezbollah 's fighters participated in the Assad Regime's last conventional offensive against the Islamic State , helping to capture al-Bukamal on the Iraqi border and nearby oil facilities on the left side of the Euphrates. Hezbollah played their role in this offensive by attacking al-Bukamal from the Iraqi side of the border, with assistance from the Iraqi PMF. From this point, all Islamic State operations on the eastern bank of the Euphrates would be guerilla and unconventional.

Fig 5. HEZBOLLAH MILITANTS ASSAULTING SARAQIB IN RIF IDLIB DURING OFFENSIVES AGAINST HTS c. MARCH OF 2020

• July 19, 2018: Hezbollah , along with elements of the IRGC, the Syrian army, and hundreds of civilians, withdrew from majority shi'ite towns of al-Fu'ah and Kafariya on a deal with HTS after more than 3 years of siege that started after rebels took Idlib. Hezbollah agreed to negotiations with the newly established HTS , which helped establish their legitimacy in the area, after an SAA offensive from the south of Aleppo had failed.

• February 5, 2020: Hezbollah faces heavy casualties during the Assad Regime's Dawn of Idlib II offensive against HTS and the rebel coalition in Idlib governorate. These casualties includes a field commander that was later buried in Southern Lebanon . Dawn of Idlib II was a decisive joint Russo-Iranian -Assadist victory that resulted in the fall of Maarat al-Nu'man, Saraqib, and alleviated pressure from Aleppo. Soon, a ceasefire was signed between the SAA and HTS , establishing a line of contact with deconflictation zones. Unfortunately, small level skirmishes and airstrikes continued across the frontline until November of 2024.

• October 14, 2021: In the aftermath of the 2019-2021 polycrisis in Lebanon , which included the largest non-nuclear explosion anywhere in the world in decades, major civil unrest and anger at the sectarian political system, and a major economic crisis worsened by COVID-19, pro-Hezbollah protesters were ambushed by alleged Christian militants, leading to a sectarian gun battle in Beirut between Hezbollah and the Lebanese Army, killing 7, including 3 Hezbollah militants. The clashes were the worst in the capital since the Hezbollah offensive in 2008.

Fig 6. HEZBOLLAH TOOPHAN IRANIAN ATGM FIRED AT AN ISRAELI MBT c. AUGUST OF 2024

• October 8, 2023: After the Palestinian group Hamas launched a devastating multi-theatre offensive in southern Israel , which killed nearly 1,200 people, tensions between Hezbollah and Israel spiked, resulting in heavy clashes and exchanges of fire across the border. During the course of nearly a year, tensions between both sides crossed into new territory unseen since 2006. Over 600 Lebanese people would be killed during this pre escalatory period. Tensions would continue to increase until Israel crossed the rubicon and invaded southern Lebanon nearly a year after that fateful day in October.

• October 1, 2024: The month of September 2024 marked perhaps one of the most eventful in Hezbollah 's history. The first large Israeli action came through the unprecedented explosion of rigged pagers and walkie talkies, killing dozens and injuring thousands. The IDF used their momentum and launched a major air campaign over southern Lebanon , killing multiple hundreds, including secretary general Hassan Nasrallah and several senior commanders, and destroying parts of the military infrastructure of Hezbollah . This campaign would culminate into a full scale IDF military invasion of Southern Lebanon through multiple axes. Due to the IDF not pushing far enough for Hezbollah to conduct real guerilla warfare as well as the preceding saturation air campaign, Hezbollah 's military capabilities were severely damaged and the IDF suffered far less casualties than they did in 2006. The strongest arm of the Axis of Resistance, nay, the jewel of the crown had never been this weak in its whole conventional history. Hezbollah and Israel would sign a ceasefire a year later, both sides forever changed by the fighting. Sporadic clashes along the border and occasional airstrikes by the IDF continued until the start of attacks on Iran in 2026 by the US and Israel , when hostilities in Lebanon resumed and intensified.

Fig 7. HEZBOLLAH FPV DRONE TARGETS IDF MERKAVA MBT IN TAYBEH, SOUTHERN LEBANON c. APRIL OF 2026

• December 7, 2024: Naim Qassem's Hezbollah silently admitted defeat and withdrew from Syria back into Lebanon after HTS seized control of Homs during their lightning offensive from Idlib that started late November and would take control of Damascus and overthrow the regime a day later. Hezbollah , weakened by months of war and bombing by Israel , was unable to do anything to save Assad and bolster the regime. The end of the Assad dynasty was also a blow to Hezbollah 's reconstruction from the earlier Israeli war, as the once infamous corridor from Iran would forever be severed.

• March 2, 2026: On February 28, 2026, Trump's United States and Israel launched a major air strike campaign across Iran , starting the largest interstate war in the Middle East since WW1. In response, Iran and its proxies launched attacks at US allies in the Persian Gulf and at Israel . Meanwhile, Hezbollah entered the war with a "sad" display of a few missiles targeted at Haifa. All were shot down. Israel retaliated, starting an air campaign even deadlier than the one to the leadup to the invasion of Lebanon in 2024. A few weeks later, Israeli troops launched a ground offensive, seizing more territory, faster, and with less casualties than during any other war with Hezbollah . Israel , who has vowed that they would create a 80s-00s type security zone in southern Lebanon , has been accused of war crimes, including the ethnic targeting of shi'ites and excessive collective punishment comparable to its campaign in Gaza (2023-present). The Fourth Lebanon War continues to this day, despite the ceasefire in Iran and ongoing Pakistani-mediated negotiations.
