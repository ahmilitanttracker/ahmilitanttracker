Tahrir al-Sham

Updated: 2/9/25

Overview

Hay'yat Tahrir al-Sham (HTS), also known as Tahrir al-Sham, is a Syrian islamist nationalist militant group created in 2017. The roots of HTS arose around the start of the insurgency phase of the war in Syria, when al-Qaeda in Iraq fighter Abu Muhammad al-Jolani crossed into Syria and created Jabhat al-Nusra. During this time, JN became known as one of the most ruthless and experienced jihadist groups fighting against Assad, attracting foreign fighters. Over the next years, JN battled with AQI (which became ISIS in 2014), changed names, and grew more nationalistic-until they left al-Qaeda, merging with other groups to form HTS in 2017. HTS battled both the Assad regime and other factions of the Syrian Opposition, solidifying their control of Idlib Governorate. In between the 2020 ceasefire and the fall of the Assad Regime, HTS' rule of Idlib Governorate was characterized by a mix of islamism, technocratic governance, and human rights violations (despite this, Idlib remained more open to dissent than the Assad regime). After a one and a half week offensive, the Assad regime fell to a combined opposition assault, that included HTS, on December 8, 2024. Jolani, now referred to as Ahmed al-Sharaa, is now promising elections in a few years as well as pluralistic governance. Despite al-Sharaa's overtures, HTS is still listed as a terrorist group by the United States, the United Nations, the United Kingdom, Russia, the European Union among others.

Ideology & Tactics

Since its split from al-Qaeda, HTS has established a unique form of islamism infused with nationalism independent from al-Qaeda or the Islamic State. This form of "Political Jihadism", a term coined by analyst Charles Lister, has been shifted and perfected throughout the 7 years of control HTS has had in the Idlib governorate. During this time, the political wing of HTS, the Syrian Salvation Government (SSG) ruled their statelet, using aspects of technocratic and shariah governance. Unlike other jihadist groups, HTS used explicitly revolutionary messaging and denounced a global jihad. They sought to endear themselves to their people as the next stage of the 2011 Syrian revolution, not an al-Qaedaesque occupation. The positions of the SSG were usually taken up by technocrats, businessmen, and academics, building a "top-down" government. The SSG would shift gears, increasing funding from taxation and business sources while phasing out foreign donations that had marked previous jihadist organizations in Syria. HTS made several appeals to Western governments, using their history in anti al-Qaeda and IS campaigns in an attempt to get out of their terrorist designation lists. On the other hand, rule by the SSG was still very much islamist and HTS still used old jihadist ties that they had gained during their time as al-Nusra. For example, in early HTS propaganda, the group often described their fight as pro-Islam and their enemies as ethnic/religious slurs. Politically, the SSG maintained a stranglehold on theological schools in Idlib, promoting salafism and rejecting western style democracy or republic (despite this they still planned some local elections in the months before their take over in 2024). The HTS forces would fight with and host transnational jihadist groups such as Ajnad al-Kavkaz and the East Turkestan Islamic Movement. HTS' relations with other rebel groups were often dicey and mixed at best. Some forces like the NFL would fight side by side HTS, while others like the Sham Legion would clash against them. While less dependent and less friendly with Turkey as the SNA was, HTS would come to accept Turkey's role as a "peacekeeper" in the Idlib region. During the ceasefire period, HTS used frequent Inghimasi, suicide, and infiltration attacks behind regime lines in response to regime violations and attacks. In a sign of shifting gears Al-Sharaa opened up military training academies in Idlib governorate, leading to a professionalization of his forces not seen in the other Syrian rebel groups. Using their own experience and tactics of other recent conflicts, HTS launched a rapid 2 week long military offensive that brought down 50 years of Assad family rule. Possibly gaining intel and support from Ukraine, HTS' Shaheen brigade (meaning falcon) used suicide, recon, and attack drones to locate and destroy Regime tanks and vehicles. The years of isolation and blockade had forced HTS to manufacture their own armored trucks and vehicles, which they used to great effect during their nationwide offensive. Now with Syria mostly under their grasp, the HTS-led government faces old but worsening issues like the economy, captagon trade, international recognition, sectarianism, and division within the non HTS controlled factions of the Syrian Army. The new Syrian government has continued to break ranks with its past, promising elections, prosecuting those who massacre Alawites, signing deals with both the Kurdish SDF and the Druze fighting forces, etc. But they have a long way to go before all of Syria is stabilized.
