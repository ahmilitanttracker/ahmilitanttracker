The Arakan Army

Updated: 2/9/25

Overview

The Arakan Army (AA) is an Arakanese ethnic armed organization located in Rakhine state. The group was established in 2009 during the Than Shwe Tatmadaw dictatorship , exiled in Kachin state. During the brief democratic period, the AA would involve itself with the KIA, before returning to Rakhine and launching an insurgent campaign. The AA would be one of many groups to break the ceasefire with the new Tatmadaw government and start offensive operations. In only a year, the AA would take large swathes of Rakhine state, encircling the capital of Sittwe and capturing the western regional command center. The relationship between the AA and the long persecuted Rohingya is complicated, with the AA often committing war crimes against the Rohingya and fighting their militant groups. The AA is listed as a terrorist group by the Tatmadaw regime .

Ideology & Tactics

The AA is one of the multiple EAOs fighting against the Tatmadaw in Myanmar. The AA believe in "Arakan Dream", an ideology based off of a self-governing Rakhine state which promises freedom, democracy, and dignity for all, including the Rohingya minority. This "way of Rakhita" seeks to restore the sovereignty of Rakhine state to the Arakanese people. However, debate has circulated whether or not this includes the historically marginalized Rohingya people, who have had villages and communities torched by AA members. Despite this belief in Rakhine nationalism, the AA support the ethnic Bamar PDF, who seek to implement a form of federalism and autonomy for minorities. In the territories conquered by the AA since late 2023, they have implemented a form of local government-representation for minorities unknown. In the past, AA forces mostly used guerilla warfare, including IED attacks, police base attacks, ambushes, and attacks on road infrastructure. Now that the Tatmadaw is on the backfoot, the AA are now using conventional and siege warfare tactics to capture and attack the remaining pockets of Junta bases & resistance in Rakhine. Internationally, the AA have not garnered enough aid to upset the worsening humanitarian crisis in Rakhine.

Notable Actions

• April 22, 2015: The Arakan Army returned from their exile in Kachin with a roar, attacking the Tatmadaw in their positions in Kyauktaw, Rakhine state.

• January 4, 2019: The Arakan Army launched a major attack against four police outposts, killing 13 and capturing 10,000 rounds of ammunition. These attacks came during the ceasefire period and a few weeks after the civilian government declared a unilateral temporary ceasefire in an attempt to negotiate with EAOs. In response, the military held a national security meeting and increased deployments across affected regions. The conflict had reignited.

• March 9, 2019: In another major attack, the Arakan Army attacked a police station in Northern Rakhine state, killing 9 policemen and capturing a handful of weapons. They also attacked and took over the commanding post of one of Rakhine's battalions.

• November 26, 2022: The AA and the Tatmadaw military Junta signed a new ceasefire following heavy fighting in Kayin and Rakhine that had broken the 2020 ceasefire. The group did not withdraw from fortifications held before the ceasefire.

• November 13, 2023: Two weeks after the Three Brotherhood Alliance launched Operation 1027 in Shan state, the Arakan Army broke its ceasefire with the Tatmadaw and attacked and seized multiple outposts, camps, and bases. More than a dozen junta soldiers were killed and 6 AA soldiers were killed on the first day of clashes. The Arakan Army 's offensive continues to this day, remaining one of the most successful rebel offensives in all of the country.

• November 14, 2023: Arakan Army forces took over the town of Pauktaw, most notable for its proximity to the regional capital and regime base of Sittwe. In response, the Tatmadaw unleashed hell from above, reducing the town to rubble and leaving many civilians in ditches. On the same day, the AA launched attacks in Paletwa township on the Chin-Rakhine border, eventually taking a major military base a few weeks later.

• January 15, 2024: After only a week's worth of fighting, the Arakan Army seized Paletwa town in nearby Chin state. Paletwa was previously an important node militarily-but also economically, as a major India -Myanmar infrastructure project ran through there. The AA also captured and arrested a brigadier general who had previously commanded missions in the Mon region.

• February 9, 2024: Arakan Army forces, after heavy fighting and the sinking of three Junta transport craft, scored their most symbolic and strategic victory yet: the capture of Mrauk U. Mrauk U was the capital and the seat of power for the last Arakanese-led state in the region. Following the fall of Mrauk U, the AA moved and consolidated its control in the district, with the fighting displacing thousands. Hundreds of Tatmadaw were killed, dying in minutes after AA soldiers struck their transport naval craft. The ruins of the old shall pave the way for the future.

• March 12, 2024: The Ramree township, which is adjacent to a Chinese port project and economic zone in Kyaukphyu, fell to the Arakan Army . Its capture was the culmination of months of heavy fighting, daily airstrikes, and artillery shelling. As Ramree township is majority Bamar, it has opened up important questions about AA tolerance and governing for non-Arakanese peoples.

• May 17, 2024: AA forces were alleged to have torched and looted the Rohingya town of Buthidaung and its satellite towns, killing 45 and resulting in the destruction of hundreds of houses. The AA also allegedly destroyed the town's hospital after it was revealed that Rohingyas were hiding in it. As a result, 200,000 Rohingyas were displaced from the region, many fleeing to the border with little supplies. The AA came out claiming that the fires were a result of recent Junta airstrikes, despite a lack of evidence.

• July 18, 2024: The Arakan Army seized the picturesque coastal town of Thandwe and its airport during their offensive moving through Rakhine state's south. Civilian casualties mounted as Junta airstrikes bombed the town indiscriminately. Following the fall of Thandwe after weeks of fighting, the AA seized the rest of the southern tip of Rakhine state and even pushed into the northern edge of Ayeyarwady.

• August 5, 2024: The Arakan Army was alleged to have launched drone strikes and artillery shelling against a column of civilians fleeing during the siege of Maungdaw. Up to 200 people were killed and many more injured. The AA officially pushed out a statement condemning the attack and assigning responsibility to the Tatmadaw . Eyewitnesses interviewed by CNN say otherwise.

• August 14, 2024: The AA , during their advance through the Rakhine state's southern tip, captured Gwa and mostly culminated that part of the offensive. To prevent the AA 's further advance, the Junta blew up an important bridge running on a river. Fighting has spilled over into nearby, mostly peaceful, Ayeyarwady state.

• December 9, 2024: The AA scored a major victory on this day, ending the six month siege of Maungdaw and capturing the entire Myanmar -Bangladesh border zone. Fighting in Maungdaw was slow and costly, with every layer of defense constructed by the Tatmadaw representing another obstacle for the AA . During the battle, Rohingya muslim militias had ironically fought alongside the Tatmadaw against the Arakan Army (many were forced), sounding many alarms in the west concerning ethnic cleansing.

• December 16, 2024: The AA seized the entirety of Taungup township, one of the last remaining Tatmadaw holdouts in Rakhine state. They took a regional military commands center and captured multiple junta soldiers.

• December 20, 2024: After nearly 3 months of fighting, the Arakan Army announced that it had captured the major town of Ann in Ann Township, Rakhine state. The AA captured the city's airport and the western regional military headquarters, representing the second of such taken by rebels in Myanmar (the first being Lashio). The fall of Ann resulted in the crippling of Tatmadaw forces in the rest of Rakhine state and their relegation to a few enclaves on the coast, including the largest city in the state, Sittwe.

• February 10, 2025: The Arakan Army and the Tatmadaw trade fire near the Junta controlled regional capital of Sittwe. According to sources close to the Irrawaddy, the Rakhine militant group is attempting to find a weakpoint in the fortified defense. As of the first of July, the AA has not moved into the city itself.

• April 25, 2025: The Three Brotherhood Alliance, including the Arakan Army , and the Junta separately declared unilateral ceasefires following the devastating earthquake, one that ended in the death of more than five thousand. Despite the detoriating humanitarian situation, both sides carried out attacks, with the Junta carrying out 409 strikes killing and injuring many civilians.

• June 9, 2025: The Arakan Army closed in on the important and strategic Sit-Tat artillery and AD hub on a hill in the road linking Rakhine and Magwe region. The base would give the AA an overarching look across the region, including the town of Padan.

• September 11, 2025: In the leadup to the sham elections in late 2025, the Tatmadaw launched major counteroffensives in multiple theatres across the country, including in Rakhine state, where they perhaps sought to reestablish a link with Kyaukphyu and Sittwe. However, unlike in Shan state, where a divided and mostly satisfied EAOs were pushed back somewhat, or Karenni state, the conscripts of the Tatmadaw were stopped completely by the AA stationed along the mountains separating Rakhine from the rest of the country, resulting in dozens of deaths.

• January 12, 2026: The AA intensified offensive operations on the Sittwe pocket, most likely preparing for a final push into the largest Tatmadaw stronghold in Rakhine state. However, during the two months of clashes, the AA were only really able to seize some outposts across the river temporarily. This is primarily because the Tatmadaw has been arming this pocket to the teeth for the past two years, meaning that even any successful offensive would likely be incredibly costly for the AA . The battle would be a high cost high reward gamble, that if won, would mean the effective end of combat operations in Rakhine state with the sole exception of a Tatmadaw protected Chinese port in the province's middle.
