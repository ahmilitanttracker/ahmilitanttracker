Proud Boys

Updated: 2/9/25

Overview

The Proud Boys nationalist is an American nationalist and neo-fascist militant group. Formed by the right wing commentator Gavin McInnes in 2011 and commanded by Enrique Tarrio, the Proud Boys gained prominence and traction during and in support of the first Trump presidency. In left wing protests/counterprotests, the PB would act like an organized street gang, engaging in fights, vandalism, and threats. The peak of their violence would arrive during the Jan. 6 insurrection, when PB members had planned out and encouraged violent attacks on Capitol officers. Following the attack, several members of the Proud Boys, including Enrique Tarrio, were arrested, causing the movement to go fairly silent. Now that Trump has pardoned Tarrio, however, this dynamic could change. Due to its engagement in political violence, the Proud Boys have been listed as a terrorist organization by Canada and New Zealand.

Ideology & Tactics

The Proud Boys arose from the increasing polarization and radicalization of American society over the 2010s. Unlike the Atomwaffen or C18, the Proud Boys believe a western chauvinist and American nationalist ideology, not always accommodating open neo-nazis. The PB are also fervently pro-Trump, attending his rallies, guarding supportive ones, and planning violent action for him during Jan. 6. The group disavows and often uses violence against social liberal causes, including LGBTQ ideology, BLM, "cultural marxism", gun regulation, and feminism. Though currently occurring less often, PB members and chapters would sometimes collaborate and meet with neo-nazi and white supremacist groups and activists. The PB operate in a decentralized form, organizing chapters in at least 35 states (including democratic states like California). The inherent decentralization of the PB organization has caused times where PB chapters condemn each other for going against neo-nazi activists. On the other hand, the fragmentation of this organization has allowed for members to join electoral politics, run for offices, and contact conservative politicians like Roger Stone. On the streets, local PB chapters have organized political rallies thoroughly, including preparations for violence, map routes, and local police precincts. PBs have acted as a "moderating force" against leftists if the police decide not to act, using clubs and tactical gear. This level of planning and willingness to commit acts of violence would culminate in the PBs involvement in the Jan. 6 insurrection, where they would push protesters to attack the police and "lead the tip of the spear" into the capitol itself. Since the attack and the subsequent jailing of its leadership, the PBs have gone mostly silent, occasionally appearing in rallies in support of Trump.

Notable Actions

• March 4, 2017: Kyle Chapman, later known as "Based Stickman" was recorded beating counter protesters with a wooden dowel during a march in Berkeley, California. The organization rallied behind him, crowdfunding money for bail and inviting him to join the organization. Chapman founded the Fraternal Order of the Alt-Knights, a subgroup of the Proud Boys , acting as a paramilitary. Later, he left on his own accord, attempting to try his own thing. Two years later, Chapman attempted to try to take over the group during the tumult of Trump's 2020 loss.

• August 12, 2017: Proud Boys member Alex Ramos assaulted DeAndre Harris with a group of other far-right members during the Unite the Right rally. Ramos was later convicted for his actions that day, sentenced to six years. Founder McInnes disavowed the event, saying that the organizer of the event, Jason Kessler, was kicked out for his views on race.

Fig 1. PB MEMBERS ATTACKING AND BRUTALIZING LEFTIST COUNTERPROTESTERS IN MANHATTAN, NEAR LEADER'S SPEECH c. AUGUST OF 2018

• August 12, 2018: Videos were shown of Proud Boy members fighting leftist counterprotesters near Manhattan’s Metropolitian Republican Club after PB founder McInnes spoke there. The meeting featured Mcinnes and an Asian member attempting to recreate the 1960 assasination of a Japanese socialist politician. The showing is an example of anticommunism surpassing white nationalism at least in this chapter’s group. During the aftermath, 10 members were arrested and a large target was placed on the group’s back. McInnes left the organization after the incident, leaving the group in shambles. Following the incident, a short power struggle occurred, eventually leading to Enrique Tarrio’s ascension to chairman.

• July 6, 2019: A total of around 300 Proud Boys marched along in Washington D.C. in the “Demand Free Speech” rally. While counterprotesters were at the ready, the police blocked any violent confrontations between the groups, allowing the rally to remain peaceful. On right wing circles, free speech is believed to constantly be under threat by left wing corporations or organizations. Thus, demanding free speech became their rallying cry.

• August 17, 2019: The Proud Boys organized the “End Domestic Terrorism” rally in Portland, Oregon. The aim of the rally was to promote the idea that Antifa and left wing protesters should be designated as terrorist organizations, an idea that was once considered during the first Trump presidency. The sight of hundreds of neo-fascists rallying with armor and flags attracted nearly a thousand left wing counter protesters, resulting in clashes that ended up with the arrests of 13.

• June 16, 2020: Members of the Proud Boys were filmed engaging in counterprotest near the Capitol Hill Occupied Protest (CHOP). Some were armed and some, like Tusitala Toese, engaged in active assault. Toese was later arrested for this incident and others on August 28, 2020.

• September 29, 2020: During one of the televised debates between Donald Trump and Joe Biden, moderator Chris Wallace asked Trump if he was willing to condemn white supremacists and militia groups. He answered that he was willing to make condemnations but asked for clarification on which group. When Biden replied with “Proud Boys”, Trump told them to “stand back and stand by”. The Proud Boys took that to heart, using the quote as a slogan.

• December 12, 2020: Tarrio and other PB members rallied near the oldest historically black church in D.C., burning the church’s Black Lives Matter sign and vandalizing the property as well as others nearby. They filmed themselves during the act, yelling “fuck antifa” and flashing white supremacist hand gestures. The attack was deemed a hate crime by police and church members. One of the churches attacked sued the group and won the rights to the “Proud Boys” trademark 5 years later. Enrique Tarrio was arrested on January 4th, 2021 (two days before Jan. 6) for charges related to the church attacks.

• January 6, 2021: A crowd whipped up by then President Donald Trump rioted and breached the grounds of America’s Capitol Building in an attempt to stop the counting of votes for incoming president Joe Biden. Hundreds if not a few thousand smashed their way through windows and doors using flags, makeshift weapons, and knives. In total, 9 people would be dead because of that day. The Proud Boys played a key role in planning the insurrection, including maps of possible attack and push in telegram channels. One such document circulated in PB chats was “1776 Returns”, a plan that included the invasion and occupation of multiple government buildings, including the capitol building. Proud Boys members led the assault, often opening up new holes for other rioters to sweep in through. The Proud Boys and other groups like the Oath Keepers collaborated during the leadup to January 6.

• June 12, 2022: A group of Proud Boys barged into a library in San Larenzo, California bay area, while a drag queen was reading a story yelling homophobic and transphobic slurs. None of the men were armed, but one of them was wearing a shirt saying “kill your local pedophile” with a drawn M4.

• June 25, 2023: A group of Proud Boys in Portland, Oregon, clashed with a local neo-nazi street group named the Rose City Nationalist Club in a anti-pride counterprotest. The decentralized nature of the organization meant that many chapters and members were overwhelmingly against this move, while others were not quite so. Many neo-nazi members declared that they would quit the movement after the event, saying that the old organization was gone. Many of them left to join active club networks.

• January 20, 2025: To mark their presence in a rapidly changing America , a group of Proud Boys marched through Washington DC to celebrate the inauguration of Donald Trump into his second term. Members shouted slogans promoting annexing Canada as the 51st state and the pardoning of their compatriots.

• January 21, 2025: Convicted seditionist and Proud Boys commander, Enrique Tarrio, was pardoned by Donald Trump along with other PB members on Jan. 6. Tarrio thanked Trump for his amnesty and said that the golden age of America had arrived.

• February 21, 2025: Now former leader Tarrio was arrested for assaulting a woman during a “press conference” outside the US Capitol with militia leaders from the Oathkeepers. He was released and charges were dropped.

• March 27, 2025: During the wave of anti-Tesla related arson and violence, counter protesters including Proud Boys were spotted defending tesla stores from leftist demonstrators. In Columbus, Ohio, a local PB chapter had called for its members to engage in protesters; overall, however, turnout was very low. Meanwhile, some counter protesters were seen with tactical gear and firearms.

• June 14, 2025: During the major nationwide No Kings Day protests, Proud Boys and other right wing nationalists engaged in counterprotest in various locations. Enrique Tarrio was also spotted, roaming the streets of Miami. In the leadup to the protests, certain chapters of the Proud Boys were sharing information about specific "atheist communist" activists and instructors for would be counterprotesters.

• January 6, 2026: A small group of Proud Boys , led by former commander Enrique Tarrio, led a march on the capital for a 5th year anniversary since the attack on the capitol building during January the 6th, 2021. While coordinators, including Tarrio, specifically said that the march was for the memory of Ashli Babbit, a rioter shot and killed when she got too close to the doors of congress, it served more as salt in the wound, reminding locals that those on Jan. 6 were ultimately successful.
