The Azov Battalion

Updated: 2/9/25

Overview

The 12th Special Operations Brigade "Azov", also known as the Azov Brigade and formally known as the Azov Battalion or regiment, is a military formation of the National Guard of Ukraine previously known to have neo-nazi and Ukrainian nationalist beliefs . Starting off as a volunteer battalion, Azov was created by the far-right politician Andriy Biletsky to fight Russian-backed separatists in the Donbass. Azov, who had gained experience during the war in Donbass, inspired its own civilian movement known as the Azov Movement. Before 2022, Azov was integrated into the Armed Forces of Ukraine and allegedly deradicalized. Despite this, Russia used Azov's neo-nazi past to justify its aggression against Ukraine . During the invasion, Azov was one of the major formations defending the besieged city of Mariupol. Due to its neo-nazi past, it took a decade before the United States lifted sanctions on the group.

Ideology & Tactics

During its time as a volunteer battalion, Azov followed a neo-nazi ideology, utilizing symbols like the black sun, the wolfsangel. The far-right ultranationalist founder of the battalion, Andriy Biletsky, often made racist and white supremacist comments. The formation was created by members of the Patriot of Ukraine party as a substitute to the weak and inept Ukrainian army during the start of the insurgency in the Donbass. During the War in Donbass, European and American neo-nazis, including the NRM and the AWD , would travel to Ukraine to fight for Azov and get military experience. Due to the group's lionization in the Donbass, supporters and members would create the Azov Movement, organizing festivals, political marches, martial arts (which is an increasing trend among the international far-right), and most worryingly of all, paramilitary training and summer camps for children. After 2014 and during the 2022 war, the now integrated (into the military) and allegedly deradicalized Azov brigade has been used as a professional NATO doctrine trained fighting force used for defense and assault.

Notable Actions

Fig 1. AZOV MILITANTS ENTER MARIUPOL IN IMPROVIZED APCS, IN A FASHION SIMILAR TO THE SYRIAN REBELS c. JUNE OF 2014

• June 13, 2014: Only a month after the volunteer formation's formation, the Azov Battalion engaged in heavy clashes with Russian separatists in Mariupol. Azov and the Dnipro-1 Battalion were able to retake the city for Ukraine , hoisting the flag over the previous headquarters of the insurgents in the city. During the battle, 2 Ukrainian soldiers were killed and five separatists were killed. Azov remained in the city, being tasked with patrolling the region to prevent arms trafficking.

• August 4, 2014: Azov fighters clash with DPR separatists in Shakhartsk Raion, moving into Mariinka, a suburb of Donetsk and a town that would later be known for complete destruction in the 2020s. Azov forces cleared houses with the help of a tank, two IFVs, and a truck. The group hoisted the Ukrainian flag over the town after its recapture. One was killed and a dozen were injured.

• August 18, 2014: The battalion advanced with Dnipro-1 and Donbas on the town of Ilovaisk, engaging in a tough urban battle with Russian separatists . The first assault failed, leading to Azov's withdrawal from the city and the end of their operations in Ilovaisk. The second wave of Ukrainians , beefed up by more reinforcements from other volunteer groups, attacked Ilovaisk and was able to seize it. Unfortunately for Kyiv, Russian regular forces crossed the border into Ukraine and quickly encircled the town of Ilovaisk. Defeated, Ukraine negotiated a truce corridor with Russia for the encircled, letting them drive back to Ukrainian-held territories . Suddenly though, the separatists opened fire on the convoys, inflicting extremely heavy casualties. By the end of the battle, anywhere from 370 to upwards of 1000 were killed and Ukraine was humiliated. Azov , meanwhile, suffered less than other volunteer formations. After that debacle, Poroshenko's government signed the Minsk I ceasefire.

• August 25, 2014: Using their lead from Ilovaisk, separatists and Russian regulars launched a major offensive to gain a portion of the Azov sea's shore. In a matter of a few days, DNR separatists captured Novoazovsk and would later advance on Mariupol, the regional epicenter. The Azov Battalion engaged with separatists and regulars , but falter towards Mariupol.

• September 4, 2014: Separatist forces advance near the regional epicenter of Mariupol. Intense clashes and shelling are reported all across the southern frontline. The Azov Battalion would be instrumental in the city's defense, holding the garrison there and training citizens on how to fight incase of a Russian offensive into the city. DPR separatists were able to push 5km away from the city center before being pushed back by Azov and Ukrainian regulars . A day later, Ukraine and Russia agreed to the Minsk I ceasefire resulting in the freezing of the frontlines. Despite this, ceasefire violations would be reported across Mariupol within the next few months. Biletsky was reportedly livid.

• November 11, 2014: The Azov Regiment (expanded from a battalion) was integrated into the Ukrainian National Guard during the Ukrainian government's efforts to reign in volunteer formations into the ATO chain of command. They had done their job, but now it was time for Kyiv to take control. It was around that time that Biletsky started to shift away from the group.

Fig 4. AZOV T-72 FIRES AS BATTALION ATTEMPTS TO ADVANCE BEYOND MARIUPOL c. FEBRUARY OF 2015

• October 15, 2014: Supporters and members of Azov and the Right Sector marched through Kyiv to commemerate the 72nd anniversary of the Ukrainian Insurgent Army , a right wing nationalist insurgent group that fought both the Soviets and Nazis during the 40s. They lit torches, waved banners, and flew flags.

• February 10, 2015: After a separatist rocket was launched on Mariupol, killing 31 civilians and injuring more than a hundred, the Azov Regiment launched a major offensive with the explicit goal of creating a buffer zone from separatist forces . Azov was able to make decent ground, pushing up to the town of Sakhanka before retreating back to Shyrokyne. Despite the Minsk II ceasefire, fighting near the town continued unabated. After months of tit for tat exchanges of fire and brutal fighting, separatist forces withdrew from the town and Azov was rotated out, replaced with Ukrainian marines . Shyrokyne eventually became a demilitarized zone.

• April 27, 2016: Some Azov fighters and vehicles were transported to Odesa to keep the peace after oblast governor (and former Georgian president during the August War) Mikheil Saakashvili warned of rising pro-Russian violence in the city.

• October 14, 2016: For the 74th anniversary of the UPA , Azov supporters and veterans marched through Kyiv and announced the formation of the National Corps party . The political party wants Ukraine to be a strong militaristic nuclear power not willing to bow down to either Moscow or Washington . During the 2019 elections, the party only won 2.15% of the vote... not enough to gain a seat.

• February 1, 2019: The Azov Regiment was deployed in the Svitlodarsk area after years of deployment away from the front. During their deployment there, Azov claimed to have inflicted severe losses on separatist forces garrisoned on the other side of the frontline.

• June 15, 2019: For the fifth anniversary of the recapture of Mariupol by Ukrainian Regulars , Azov members , National Guard members , and members of other formations participated in a parade and celebration in Mariupol, attended directly by newly inaugurated president Volodymyr Zelensky.

Fig 5. THE DESTROYED RUINS OF AZOVSTAL, PREVIOUSLY ONE OF THE LARGEST STEEL WORKS IN EUROPE, WHERE AZOV FIGHTERS WERE HELD UP IN c. APRIL OF 2022

• March 2, 2022: On February 24th, 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine , shocking the world. One of the invasion's largest initial goals was the linking up of forces from Donetsk and Crimea in a sort of land bridge, one might say. Through that bridge, Mariupol lay. As before in 2014 and 2015, the Azov Regiment stood proudly to defend this city. Azov and other Ukrainian formations defended the port city from the Russian forces for over 80 days, slowly being pushed back and back before surrendering in the Azovstal plant. Azov's role in the defense of the city helped preoccupy thousands of Russian soldiers who could be used to advance somewhere else. By the end of the siege, thousands of civilians had been killed and a vast majority of the city was destroyed. It took years of negotiations and deals to return Azov soldiers and commanders from Russian POW camps, with some still in custody. From this point, concerns over the ideology of members of the Azov Brigade were brushed away.

• January 26, 2023: The Azov Regiment's SSO (specops) unit , which was composed of formations that were created outside of Mariupol, was restructured into the Third Separate Assault Brigade , serving under the Ukrainian Ground Forces . This unit was deployed to Bakhmut and has remained active ever since. The Azov that had fought in Mariupol was expanded into a brigade of the new Offensive Guard.

• August 17, 2023: After a year of reconstruction after Mariupol, the Azov Brigade returned to the front fighting in Luhansk Oblast near the city of Kremmina. This time, August of 2023, was also during the southern Ukrainian counteroffensive , which Azov also contributed in earlier. Though it made minor gains, Ukraine was unable to significantly take major ground in its Offensive, mostly due to heavy Russian fortifications and limited western aid. The war would go on.

Fig 6. AZOV FIGHTERS CLEARING PARTS OF THE TORETSK SECTOR c. 2024

• February 7, 2024: The Azov Brigade engaged in tough combat operations in the Kreminna sector, operating to slow the Russian advance . They used drones and small counterattacks to stall the Russians out. Though, they would later come back and take the settlement a year later.

• September 2, 2024: The Azov Brigade was transferred from Lyman near the north of the front to Toretsk and New York, towns and cities located in the center of Donetsk Oblast. While they sustained heavy losses, the frontline was stabilized relatively. In the link attached to this text, an Azov commander expressed his disapproval for current Ukrainian strategy in that front and in Kursk. This offensive was part of a larger Russian push to take the important logistical node and city of Pokrovsk in Donetsk Oblast. To this day, the Azov Brigade remains active fighting in this sector.

• December 23, 2024: The Azov Brigade announced the creation of a new international battalion, aimed at attracting new foreign volunteers and bolstering the ranks of the formation. Due to the brigade's long and troubled history with far-right ideology and its heroic history in Mariupol, it became an attractive option for foreign fighters. Perhaps fighting in Azov is the modern far-right equivalent of signing up for fighting the USSR in Afghanistan ?

Fig 7. AZOV DRONE VIDEOS DESTRUCTION OF RUSSIAN CONVOY DURING THEIR FAILED DOBROPILLIA OFFENSIVE c. OCTOBER OF 2025

• April 15, 2025: The Azov Brigade was morphed and brought under the new 1st Corps of the Ukrainian National Guard "Azov", the first of such formations in the Ukrainian National Guard . The corps includes 4 other brigades and is commanded by the former leader of Azov and defender of Mariupol, Denys Prokopenko. This move was done to increase the effectiveness and cohesiveness of structure. A new commander was appointed for the brigade itself-Bohdan Hrishenkov.

• August 13, 2025: Russian forces , now focused on seizing Pokrovsk and Myrnohrad in Donetsk Oblast, were able to strike a successful breakthrough through undefended fortifications in the north of that direction. Russia gained a significant amount of territory in a small amount of time, even threatening Dobropillya. Ukraine scrambled a response, sending over Azov to the region. Eventually, the Ukrainians were able to push back, encircle, and destroy the breakthrough.
