Hamas

Updated: 2/9/25

Overview

Hamas, officially known as the Islamic Resistance Movement, is a Palestinian islamist nationalist political group with a militant wing known as the al-Qassam Brigades. Since its inception in the First Intifada, Hamas has come to capture the entire Gaza Strip and parts of the West Bank from the IDF and the Militants Authority . Unlike the ruling party, Fatah , Hamas has continued armed resistance against Israel , launching missile, rocket, and suicide bomb attacks against both military and civilian targets. Politically, Hamas has gained popularity as the largest organization opposed to Fatah and PA political and economic corruption. Since Hamas took control of Gaza from the PA , politically The insurgent war that Hamas is currently waging against Israel started after the October 7th attacks, the culmination of planning and training by the Qassam Brigades, using every tactic that they have utilized in the past. Due to its violent methods, Hamas and its militant wing have been listed as terrorist organizations by the European Union, Israel , the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, among others.

Ideology & Tactics

The ideology of Hamas, the largest Palestinian islamist nationalist group, primarily stems from its manifestos written in 2017 and 1988. In its 2017 manifesto, the political branch of Hamas stated that it wanted a return to 1967 borders, full control of al-Aqsa and Jerusalem, the right of return, among others. In comparison to its 1988 manifesto, Hamas dropped the virulent antisemitic rhetoric, instead saying that their problem is with the "Zionist project" but not the Jews. Since Hamas descends from the Muslim Brotherhood, they also believe in a virulent islamism, promoting jihad and unity with the Islamic ummah. But this does not mean Hamas is a salafi jihadist organization, since they still believe deeply in Palestinian nationalism, fighting against anti-nationalist pan-islamist groups like al-Qaeda and ISIS. Hamas leadership can generally be divided into two separate categories: outside of Gaza and inside of Gaza. Usually, Qassam leadership in Gaza, like Yahya Sinwar, is seen as more fanatical and aggressive than Hamas leadership in Doha, Qatar. However, the extent of how much control the political leadership has over the militant leadership, and how serious of a schism (if it exists at all) there was between the political and militant leadership before October 7 is still hotly debated. Tactically, before October 7th and the start of the largest round of tensions with Israel , Hamas most commonly used hybrid war and "terroristic" methods. During both Intifadas, Hamas militants would pioneer suicide bombing (sometimes using children), mass shootings, and rocket/mortar attacks against both Israeli civilian and military targets. These hardline tactics would further embolden the Israeli right and further push aside the remaining credibility of the peace process. Politically, during this time, Hamas would blame the PA and the Fatah administration for increasing corruption and the failing of the Oslo peace process. This tactic allowed them to sweep the 2006 Palestinian electons, winning in Gaza and wrestled control of the strip from PA security forces. Now as a statelet, Hamas would circumvent the newly created Israeli and Egyptian blockade by creating a vast labyrinth of tunnels, often dubbed the "Gaza Metro". The "Metro" would be used to smuggle in much needed staples like food and medicine, but also money and illicit goods like firearms and rockets from regional partners like Iran, nudging Hamas into the Axis of Resistance. Hamas understood how strong and effective youth recruitment can be, establishing youth summer camps and TV programs for the indoctrination of children. During Hamas' occupation of Gaza and the buildup to 10/7, Israel and her allies accused the group operating in Gaza of stealing foreign aid and repurposing it for militant use. Perhaps because of this, or due to the blockade, the Gaza Strip became extremely impoverished and the youth suffered heavily due to unemployment. The IDF and the Qassam Brigades engaged in various short but hot wars in the years before October 7, 2023, an attack that included all previous Hamas tactics and culminated years of planning. On October 7th, Hamas infiltrated the Gaza "Envelope" through land, sea, and air, taking hundreds hostage, and killing more than a thousand Israeli civilians. In Israel 's subsequent offensive and ground war in Gaza, Hamas units have used guerilla warfare, blending into the destroyed landscape. During combat operations, fighters would engage in decentralized warfare, popping up over and over again in areas previously cleared by the IDF .

Notable Actions

• May 3, 1989: In one of their first militant attacks during the chaos of the First Intifada, Hamas militants kidnapped and killed two Israeli soldiers in southern Israel , perhaps around Gaza. The bodies were recovered years later during the mid 1990s. Ahmed Yassin, the first chairman of Hamas , was arrested and sentenced for life in prison. But he went back to the same rhetoric after his release was negotiated during an agreement with Jordan.

• April 16, 1993: In the first SVBIED attack of the Israeli -Palestinian conflict, A Hamas militant detonated a Volkswagen van in an Israeli settlement in the West Bank, killing one Palestinian civilian and injuring his brother and eight Israeli soldiers. Truly a revolution in militant tactics.

• April 6, 1994: In retaliation for the Kahanist terrorist attack in Palestinian Hebron, West Bank, A Hamas militant detonated himself in a northern bus near Nazareth, killing eight civilians and injuring 55. Many of the injured and one of the killed were Arab civilians. Later, another suicide bombing by a Hamas militant occurred for the same reason, killing five. A Shin Bet advisor believed that the Cave of the Patriarchs massacre was the final straw, which brought a clear motive for Hamas to launch attacks against civilians in Israel .

• October 19, 1994: A Hamas suicide bomber detonated himself in a bus going along the streets of Tel Aviv, killing 22 and injuring more than a hundred. The bus was turned into "blackened skeleton" as described by the NYT with bodies strewn across the ground. The ingredients needed for the military grade TNT was either brought in via smuggling or by Israeli organized crime.

• February 25, 1996: A Palestinian militant, who was later claimed by Hamas and trained up by Iran, detonated himself inside of a packed bus in the Jaffa Road of Jerusalem, killing 26 and injuring 48. Some days later, another Palestinian militant detonated himself in the same place, killing 19 and injuring 7. One of those who were killed in the first attack came to study in Jerusalem from America .

• July 30, 1997: Two Palestinian militants claimed by Hamas would detonate themselves in two consecutive bombings in a market in Jerusalem, killing 16 and injuring 178. The attack, which resulted in destruction of the market, was condemned by Yasser Arafat and As a result of the terrorist attack, Mossad agents attempted to poison and kill the Hamas political leader Khaled Mashal.

• June 1, 2001: In one of the deadliest Hamas attacks during the second Intifada, a suicide bomber exploded in a disco club in Tel Aviv, killing 21 and injuring 120, many of them teenagers. The terrorist responsible was taunting and mocking the young Israelis , repeatedly saying that "something's gonna happen". Arafat, the UN, and Bush condemned the attacks.

• August 9, 2001: A Hamas suicide bomber exploded in a Sbarro Pizzeria in Israeli occupied east Jerusalem, killing 16 and injuring 140. The restaurant was reportedly filled with customers, including women, children, and babies. The attack was reportedly done in retaliation of an IDF strike that killed two Hamas commanders in the West Bank. The Israeli government has claimed that the PA was warned about the ill intentions of the militant.

• March 27, 2002: On the eve of the holy Jewish day of passover, a Palestinian Hamas militant detonated a explosive device carried in a suitcase, killing 30 and injuring 160 civilians. The terrorist attack affected mainly elderly persons, including many survivors of the Holocaust. The attack was condemned by Yasser Arafat, who ordered the arrests of the attack's planners. In response, the Israeli government declared an emergency and launched Operation Defensive Shield, an attempt to defang Palestinian militancy in West Bank. Like the operations before and after it, however, it failed to provide real results.

• March 31, 2002: A Palestinian suicide bomber detonated inside of the Matza restaurant in the northern Israeli city of Haifa, killing 16 and injuring over 40. The attack was claimed by Hamas , who stated that they would continue to launch attacks until the IDF withdrew from Palestinian lands. The restaurant was a popular destination for city residents, including the many Arabs who worked there.

• March 5, 2003: A Hamas suicide bomber detonated himself inside of a bus in Haifa, killing 17 and injuring 53. Israeli police claimed that the bomb was covered with metal shrapnel for maximizing injuries. In response to the terrorist attack, the IDF killed a senior Hamas leader in Gaza, provoking condemnation and a vow for continued attacks. According to Israeli government, the bomber praised the 9/11 attacks that had just happened less than two years before.

• June 10, 2007: Following their victory in the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections, tensions between establishment Fatah and Hamas exploded, resulting in heavy political deadlock, with both sides rejecting a government by the other. This deadlock boiled over into an open civil war, with heavy fighting starting across the Palestinian territories of Gaza and the West Bank. In a matter of five days, Hamas fighters seized control of the entire Gaza Strip from Fatah . Both sides were accused of war crimes like the targeting of civilians and engaging in warfare around hospitals. After the conclusion of the short civil war, both Hamas and Fatah established separate governments over Palestine, forever splitting the Palestinian cause.

• December 27, 2008: A shaky ceasefire between Israel and Hamas collapsed after the IDF launched an operation in order to destroy a Hamas tunnel in central Gaza. The result was a tense period of airstrikes and rocket attacks that would culminate into the a major air campaign and the start of Operation Cast Lead. After softening up Hamas infrastructure, the IDF launched a ground offensive, moving into the heart of the city from the area of former Netzarim settlement and from the east through Beit Hanoun, causing a significant humanitarian crisis. They stopped dead before going into Khan Younis. In response, Hamas employed unconventional tactics, using booby-trapped houses, tunnels, rampant use of IEDs, and rampant use of rockets. As the blockade on the Gaza Strip tightened, the group relied more on domestically produced anti-tank weapons like the al-Battar and Banna 1. The IDF and Hamas agrees to a ceasefire, ending the war that killed more than a thousand Palestinians (mostly civilians), but only 10 Israeli soldiers. While a tactical Israeli victory, Hamas ' performance during the 2008 war helped to solidify its reign and gain legitimacy in the Gaza Strip, both internally and abroad.

• August 14, 2009: Heavy fighting erupts in the southern Gazan city of Rafah after a jihadist cleric by the name of Abdel Latif Moussa declared an Islamic Emirate of Rafah during a sermon. He called Hamas un-islamic and pledged allegiance to Bin Laden's al-Qaeda. Shortly after its conclusion, Qassam militants stormed the mosque and various other points in Rafah, killing 15 jihadists including their founders. By the next day, most of the group's fighters had been destroyed.

• August 31, 2010: /Article.aspx?id=186614"> Hamas militants opened fire on Israeli civilians driving on an illegal route through West Bank civilians, killing four. Khaled Mashal vowed more attacks if Israel did not return to 1967 borders, saying that they would continue to kill illegal settlers. In response, the IDF launched a new series of attacks in the West Bank, killing senior Hamas members and a commander. The attacks hindered the then ongoing peace talks between the PA and Israel .

• November 14, 2012: -military-chief-killed-in-Gaza-air-strike.html"> A month of tensions between Hamas and Israel , started initially by fishing right tensions and later exacerbated by an attack on IDF soldiers in the West Bank, spiralled out into full out war as the IDF announced the retaliation to that attack in the Gaza Strip. Operation Pillar of Defense started with a bang, killing Ahmed Jabari, a senior militant often considered second in command. Over the next week, the Israeli airforce launched dozens of airstrikes daily, killing dozens of Palestinian civilians and militants alike . In response, Hamas and other groups launched dozens of rockets at Israeli cities, with most getting shot down by the iron dome, but some hitting their targets and injuring some. Both sides heavily utilized propaganda through social media for the first time in that conflict. After a week, both sides agreed to a ceasefire mediated by the Egyptians and Americans, ending the operation. By its end, 6 Israelis were killed compared to 160 Palestinians, mostly civilians. The war led to little change.

• June 12, 2014: Palestinian militants kidnapped three teenage Jewish Israeli settlers in the West Bank, causing the sparks that would explode into the 2014 Gaza War. Despite Hamas leadership not claiming responsibility, the IDF launched a major search operation and crackdown on their networks in both Gaza and the West Bank, leading to airstrikes and rocket attacks. The pounding and the inability of either side on deescalation would lead to Israel launching Operation Protective Edge. This time, unlike in 2012, the IDF succeeded the pounding of the Gaza Strip with a ground invasion, mostly centered around Gaza City's suburb of Shujayyah. In retaliation, Hamas fired over 4,500 rockets into Israel , one of the highest totals for a single war so far. Militants also attempted to employ Gaza's vast tunnel network to use offensively, but most tries ultimately failed. In this conflict, Hamas employed a variety of weapons, including ATGMs, sniping, and interestingly, the addition of small quantities of MANPADS smuggled in by tunnel. But yet again, neither side was willing to risk it all, resulting in a peace deal that would change little from the pre-war situation. By the end of the 55 days, 67 Israeli soldiers were killed and over 2,000 Palestinians killed, atleast a majority were civilians, marking both the deadliest war so far for Palestinians but also for Israeli soldiers. The issues of 2014 would influence future Hamas decision making in the leadup to October 7.

• May 10, 2021: In 2021, following the Israeli storming of the al-Aqsa mosque and the upcoming eviction of Palestinian families from the Sheikh Jarrah neighborhood of Jerusalem, Hamas sent out an ultimatum, demanding a reversal of these policies or they and the Palestinian resistance would attack. Hamas ' threat of force was backed up with 250 rockets coming from Gaza, killing two Israeli women. In response, Israel launched a major airstrike and missile campaign against targets across the Gaza Strip, starting the deadliest war of the Israeli -Palestinian conflict since 2014. By the installment of the ceasefire a few weeks later, Hamas had launched more than 4,000 projectiles at Israel , resulting in the deaths of 14 civilians and one soldier. Meanwhile, 256 Palestinians, including civilians and militants , were killed in Gaza. During the conflict, Hamas attempted to pull of both suicide drone and ground incursions into Israel , but ultimately failed. Neither side made much ground.

• October 7, 2023: After around nine years of increasing buildup, Hamas in conjunction with an assortment of other Palestinian groups launched Operation al-Aqsa Flood, a brutal and coordinated attack on Israel 's Gaza Envelope. Hamas operatives struck and infiltrated through the sea, air, and land, crossing the border and attacking kibbutzim, cities, and even a music festival. This attack was coordinated with a large scale rocket campaign, with multiple hitting Tel-Aviv. After two days of unabated infiltration, Hamas fighters had killed over 1,100 Israelis and had kidnapped hundreds more. This event whipped up Netanyahu's far right government into a frenzy, with them launching a full-scale invasion of Gaza 20 days later. Since then, the Gaza Strip has been subjected to one of the worst humanitarian crises in modern history, killing atleast 70,000 (though the real number may be higher than 100,000), displacing millions inside of the Strip, destroying 70% of buildings in the Strip in one of the worst bombing campaigns in modern history, and resulting in mass starvation and epidemics. The destruction in Gaza has been so bad that numerous human rights agencies, including a UN committee, have concluded that Israel is carrying out a genocide in Gaza. While catching Israel completely by surprise, the attack was not coordinated with the rest of the Axis of Resistance in mind, resulting in a delayed response from the rest of the coalition. This would provide fatal as America and Israel would surgically strike and damage the rest of the network, one by one. October 7 fundamentaly shifted the dynamics of the Israel -Palestinian conflict and the middle east as a whole. The region would never be the same.

• December 13, 2023: Hamas forces opened small arms and grenade fire on a squad of Golani Brigade soldiers who attempted to enter a building in Shujayyah, a neighborhood in Gaza city. Seven were killed and four were injured in the initial attack. With many concerns that those who were dead or injured may be captured and thrown into nearby tunnels, an IDF search and rescue team was dispatched to the building. When attempting to rescue the injured, Hamas opened fire and killed two more. In total, nine were killed, resulting in one of the deadliest days for Israeli forces in Gaza.

• January 22, 2024: In Maghazi refugee camp in central Gaza, a Hamas militant fired an RPG round at a tank, triggering explosives nearby that were set to detonate and destroy two buildings. The explosion killed 21 Israeli soldiers and injured several more, resulting in one of the deadliest single days for the Israeli military during their campaign in Gaza. IDF search and rescue personnel worked for several hours to bring the men out of the rubble. No group directly took credit for the attack.

• April 6, 2024: Hamas kills 4-9 IDF personnel in an ambush in Khan Younis. The militants arose from a tunnel and started firing at an Israeli patrol coming along their logistics route in Southern Gaza. The escape out of the ambush was booby trapped as well. A Merkava entered the battlefield, forcing the militants to withdraw. This ambush was fully filmed and published by the Qassam Brigades, with them titling it the "Ambush of the Righteous".

• June 15, 2024: %20fighters%20killed%20eight%20Israeli,force%20deployed%20to%20the%20scene"> Al-Qassam Brigade fighters conducted an ambush against a convoy of IDF military vehicles north of Rafah in the suburb of Tell as-Sultan, killing eight soldiers. It started with a Hamas squad launching an RPG attack against an Israeli military bulldozer, destroying the vehicle and killing a number of soldiers. Hamas forces then attacked a rescue vehicle with their RPGs, destroying the vehicle and killing everyone inside. Hamas declared responsibility for the attack. This attack came as the IDF continued their offensive on the Philadelphi corridor during their general offensive in Rafah.

• August 31, 2024: Amid the IDF 's Operation "Summer of the Camps", Hamas gunmen in Jenin killed one and wounded 3 Israeli personnel as they attempted to find and kill a senior Qassam officer in the region. The IDF continued the operation for more than two months, killing more than two dozens Palestinian militants . The operation yielded little results.

• October 1, 2024: Nearly a year after the October 7th terrorist attack, Hamas gunmen opened fire in the Jerusalem Boulevard of Tel Aviv, killing 7 civilians and injuring 17. The attack started at a mosque that was held hostage and continued at a light rail station. Both terrorists were killed by a security guard and passerby. The attack was the deadliest incident of terrorism since October 7th.

• December 5, 2024: The Palestinian Authority launched Operation "Protect the Homeland", attacking and attempting to seize the Jenin refugee camp from militant groups, including PIJ and Hamas . Interestingly, the PA blamed these groups for provoking Israeli kahanists and resulting in Palestinian suffering, claims that the militants clapped back on. Eventually, the PA failed to sufficiently crack down on Hamas and the PIJ in Jenin, leading to a much larger IDF operation.

• January 21, 2025: After seeing the PA 's failed operation in Jenin and its refugee camp, the Israelis launched Operation "Iron Wall", moving ground soldiers, choppers, and armored vehicles into Jenin, Tulkarm, and Nur Shams, clearing their refugee camps of militant groups, including Hamas . While it made much better progress than "Summer of the Camps", "Iron Wall" was built off of the mass displacement of Palestinians from these refugee camps. On the other hand, violence against Palestinians from Israeli settlers have exploded as a result from the weakening of the militant groups.

• April 6, 2025: A Hamas sniper east of Beit Hanoun reportedly killed four Israeli soldiers east of Beit Hanoun in an operation reportedly called "Breaking the Swords" (a reference to the IDF 's Operation Iron Swords). The ambush also included the targeting of armored vehicles and jeeps. The attack was filmed by the Qassam Brigades, publishing it later as propaganda.

• June 6, 2025: Amidst the IDF 's most recent offensive/raid on Khan Younis in the southern Gaza Strip, Hamas executed an ambush that killed five IDF personnel. An IDF squad set off an explosion as they were attempting to clear a booby trapped building, causing it to collapse and descend onto the soldiers. Hamas claimed responsibility for this attack.

• July 7, 2025: Amid the start of heavy Israeli military operations in the Northern Gaza Strip, especially in Beit Hanoun, Hamas militants executed an ambush, killing five IDF personnel. The militants activated IEDs while Israeli forces were passing by, resulting in casualties. Then the Hamas militants opened fire on Israeli forces attempting to rescue their brethren. They used heavy machine guns moved up Beit Hanoun's nearby hills without being detected. After a few more weeks of brutal tit for tat insurgency, the IDF announced that Beit Hanoun was cleared of all militants by the start of August.

• September 8, 2025: Two Palestinian militants from the West Bank opened fire on civilians at a busy junction in the Israeli controlled area in Jerusalem, killing 6 and injuring 21. With Hamas claiming responsibility for the attack, saying it was a natural response for Israeli war crimes and alleged genocide in Gaza, the Israelis stepped up operations in the West Bank, further escalating "Operation Iron Wall".

• September 16, 2025: After weeks of buildup and signalling, the IDF launched a major offensive on Gaza city, seeking to finally seize it and clear it of Hamas militants. The IDF made steady progress, eventually cutting off the city of Gaza and engaging Hamas and Palestinian groups in urban combat in multiple city districts. The offensive was widely condemned across dozens of countries, as it threatened total humanitarian catastrophe in the strip. However, the pressure of multiple nations, including Trump's USA , stopped this offensive half way. A ceasefire was signed, one that would stick much better than previous ones during the Gaza war. The IDF withdrew behind a "Yellow line" that spanned across the Gaza Strip.

• October 12, 2025: Hamas militants moved back into Gaza City and cracked down heavily on the rival Doghmush clan following a ceasefire with Israel , killing more than 25. In fact, across the strip, Hamas would clash with rival Palestinian groups, often built up by Israel , such as the Popular Forces and the Counter-Terrorism Strike Force. Perhaps if the IDF were to withdraw from Gaza, it could rely on these militias set up below the yellow line to stave off Hamas .
